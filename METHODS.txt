POOLED RNAi COMPETITION EXPERIMENT AND PHENOTYPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A pool of wild isolates was grown on bacteria expressing double-stranded RNA
against each of ten target genes (fog-2, mig-6, pos-1, rde-3, ric-3, rpn-12,
spe-19, spe-43, unc-39, vha-5) and on the HT115 empty-vector control, and the
pool was sequenced at two timepoints. [TO FILL: pool composition and how it was
assembled, generations of competition, timepoint definitions, replicate
structure, DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Per-strain phenotypes were derived from the timepoint-2 frequencies and are
distributed in data/pooled_RNAi_expt/reanalysis/vst_association_traits.csv in
three parameterisations per target: delta_ctrl_<gene>_T2, the RNAi-minus-control
change in pool frequency; log2fc_<gene>_T2, its log2 ratio; and
vst_<gene>_T2, a variance-stabilised transform. All association mapping and all
phenotype figures in this manuscript use the vst columns, so the Manhattan
panels and the phenotype panels are on the same scale.

Nine of the 93 strains in the pool have no vst value and were dropped, leaving
n = 84 for association mapping.

[TO FILL: the definition of the variance-stabilising transform. It is not
reproducible from this repository -- only its output is distributed -- and the
transform matters for the interpretation of effect sizes.]

The 2023 pos-1 experiment was analysed separately; its full analysis lives
outside this repository at /UCLA/Projects/bulkGWAS/lipid_RNAi/2023_pos1 and only
its exported end products are used here
(data/pos1_original/updated_analysis/). Sample-level frequencies are
distributed at three read-depth cutoffs; cutoff 5 was used throughout, because
taking the mean pos-1 delta per strain at cutoff 5 reproduces the shipped
delta_ctrl_pos-1_T2 to 2.5e-16, against r = 0.9998 at cutoff 3 and r = 0.9988 at
cutoff 10 (scripts/2023_pos1_analysis.R).

One strain, JU1793, appears twice per sample in that dataset at every depth
cutoff and in both arms, with one row carrying the frequency and the other
approximately 1e-21 -- non-negative least squares splitting one strain's
abundance across two identical entries in the genotype reference. Rows sharing
a (strain, sample) key were therefore summed before deltas were taken, which
approximately doubles JU1793's pos-1 delta, from 0.00857 to 0.0171, and is a
no-op for every other strain. This matters because JU1793 is a cross parent.
The shipped vst values and the association scan were used as distributed and
still carry the averaged JU1793 value.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STRAIN-FREQUENCY DECONVOLUTION FROM POOLED SEQUENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Strain frequencies were recovered from pooled sequence by non-negative least
squares against a genotype reference matrix. Markers with any missing genotype
call were dropped rather than zero-filled, because a zero is a real
homozygous-reference call in this encoding and imputing one would alter the
design matrix (in the Baugh reference matrix used for validation, all 1,237,106
markers are complete, so the filter is a no-op there). Writing G for the
genotype matrix and a for the per-sample allele counts, the normal-equation
inputs G'a and G'G were formed and solved per sample with RcppML::nnls, and each
sample's solution was normalised to sum to one
(scripts/Figure1_common.R, baugh_frequencies()).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VALIDATION OF THE DECONVOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The deconvolution was validated three times, each holding a different quantity
known: in simulation, where the input frequencies and the counts are both
synthetic; on a designed DNA mixture, where the input is known and the counts
are real; and against a published independent measurement of the same samples,
where neither is controlled. They are reported in that order.

Simulation. To determine whether non-negative least squares (NNLS) regression
can infer strain frequencies from pooled populations, we established a
simulation framework. Each wild isolate was assigned a fitness value drawn from
an inverse chi-squared distribution; the expected pooled allele frequencies such
a population would produce were computed from the CeNDR genotype matrix; and
observed alt-allele counts were simulated by binomial sampling at 1, 3, 5, 10,
30, 50, 100 and 500x. Those counts were deconvolved back to per-strain
frequencies by NNLS and compared with the known input. Seven published
C. elegans traits with validated QTL supplied the fitness structure
(Albendazole_q75.TOF, PC1, assay_norm, mtDNA_ratio, value, amsacrine_f.L1 and
etoposide_median.TOF), giving seven independent simulated populations of 327
strains. Recovery was quantified as the r-squared of estimated against known
input frequency at each depth (scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_simulation_depth.R). All
seven traits reach r-squared 1.00 by 500x. At 1x, recovery ranges from 0.91
(PC1) to 0.52 (mtDNA_ratio), median 0.79; 10x is sufficient for five of the
seven traits, and 30x is the lowest depth at which all seven sit at or above
0.95.

Designed DNA mixture. 174 wild isolates were divided into four sets of roughly
equal size (A, B, C, D). Genomic DNA from each set was pooled and sequenced both
pure -- three libraries per set -- and as a seven-step titration of set B
against set C. Alt-allele counts were taken with GATK ASEReadCounter, markers
were restricted to those with mean depth across samples above 5 and below 100,
high-frequency variants were recoded so that the minor allele is counted
throughout, sites with any missing genotype were dropped, and the resulting
counts were deconvolved by NNLS (scripts/legacy/dilution_experiment.R;
scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_dilution_validation.R). Pure pools returned 0.775 to 0.853
of their own set, with 0.020 to 0.127 misassigned to each other set. Across the
titration, set B rose monotonically from 0.12 to 0.72 and set C fell
monotonically from 0.70 to 0.10 (Spearman rho +1 and -1 against titration step),
while the two untitrated sets stayed flat. Restricting the genotype reference to
the 84 strains of sets B and C and renormalising gives complementary shares
running from 0.17:0.83 to 0.84:0.16.

[TO FILL: the intended B:C mixing proportions for the seven titration steps, and

Per-strain resolution was related to genetic similarity as follows. A strain
absent from a pool must be assigned a frequency of zero in that pool's
libraries whatever the DNA input was, so for each strain the mean frequency
assigned across the nine libraries of the three sets it does not belong to was
taken as an assumption-free error measure ("leakage"). Genetic similarity was
summarised as identity-by-state -- the fraction of markers at which two strains
carry the same allele -- to the closest other strain in the reference, computed
over 231,615 variable markers sampled under a fixed seed from the CeNDR
genotype matrix. Leakage rises with nearest-neighbour identity (Spearman
rho = 0.326, p = 1.4e-05, n = 170), while the fraction of its own pool a strain
recovers does not (rho = 0.046, p = 0.55). Genetic similarity therefore predicts
misassignment into pools a strain is absent from, without predicting how well
that strain's own share is recovered.

One isotype is assigned to two sets in this experiment: strains JU1580 and
JU1793 share the isotype JU1793 and were placed in sets B and D respectively.
Because the deconvolution operates at isotype level and returns a single
frequency for the pair, that estimate is attributable to either set; it is
counted in set B throughout, which is both what the original analysis did and
what the estimate's behaviour across the titration indicates.

Accuracy was assessed against published multiplexed inverse PCR sequencing
(MIP-seq) frequencies for the Baugh L1 starvation time course, the one dataset
in which both measurements exist for the same samples (102 strains, 23 samples).

For each strain and replicate arm, the rate of change in pool frequency was
taken as the slope of the regression of frequency change on day, with day 1 as
the baseline and day 17 excluded, and slopes were averaged over the five
replicate arms. Slopes were computed in closed form
(sum((x - xbar) y) / sum((x - xbar)^2)), which is identical to the least-squares
coefficient. N2 was excluded from all comparisons: as the reference strain its
genotype shares sites with every other strain in the pool, so its frequency is
not identified on the same footing as the wild isolates'. Agreement was
quantified by Spearman correlation of the two platforms' slopes across strains
(n = 98, rho = 0.97, p < 1e-4) and, separately, per sample across strains
(23 samples, median rho = 0.84, minimum 0.517).

Uncertainty on the slopes was obtained by repeating the entire slope
calculation inside each of 100 bootstrap replicates of the deconvolution --
delta against the day-1 sample of the same replicate arm, regression on day,
average over arms -- rather than by propagating a stored standard error through
that chain, and the reported interval is the percentile interval across
replicates. Each replicate supplies its own baseline, so the covariance between
baseline and later timepoints is preserved. The bootstrap distribution of the
Spearman correlation itself is rho = 0.972, 95% interval [0.969, 0.975]. That
the array resamples the plotted estimator, and that the transformation is
carried through it consistently, was verified at four points
(scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_bootstrap_propagation_checks.R): bootstrap mean against
point estimate at the frequency level (r = 0.999984, n = 2,346) and at the slope
level (r = 0.999994, n = 101); a standardised bias of median -0.029 bootstrap
SD; and coverage of 101 of 101 intervals containing their own point estimate.

[TO FILL: what the bootstrap resampled -- reads, markers or strains. Only the

Sequencing-depth requirements were assessed by subsampling reads to 0.25, 0.5,
1, 3, 5 and 10x, rerunning the deconvolution and recomputing the slopes
(scripts/baugh_L1_DownSample_Counts.R). Agreement with the MIP-seq slopes
saturates by 3x (Spearman rho 0.833, 0.870, 0.851, 0.903, 0.906 and 0.904 across
the six depths). At the level of individual samples rather than slopes the
median rises from 0.724 to 0.827 over the same range, and the spread between
samples (roughly 0.43 to 0.88 at any fixed depth) exceeds the effect of depth
(scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_downsample_per_sample.R).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLE-TESTING THRESHOLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two thresholds are reported on every association panel. The first is Bonferroni
over every tested marker, which treats markers as independent. They are not:
these panels carry long haplotype blocks, so that threshold is
over-conservative. The second divides alpha = 0.05 by the effective number of
independent tests estimated from the eigenvalues of the marker correlation
matrix by the method of Li and Ji (2005), in which each eigenvalue lambda
contributes 1{lambda >= 1} + (lambda - floor(lambda)).

Computing the M x M marker correlation matrix directly is infeasible at these
marker counts. Instead the non-zero eigenvalues of that matrix were obtained
from the n x n matrix A = Zc Zc' / (n - 1), where Zc is the column-standardised
genotype matrix; the two matrices share their non-zero spectrum. Missing calls
were mean-imputed per marker rather than dropped, because dropping any marker
with a missing call removed about 40% of markers and silently changed the
denominator; the Bonferroni threshold is computed over the same marker set.
trace(A) = M was asserted at run time as a correctness check
(scripts/eigen_independent_tests.R; accessor in scripts/gwas_thresholds.R).

  panel              markers    M_eff   Bonferroni   Li & Ji
  pooled_RNAi_expt   322,010      732      6.81       4.17
  pos1_2023          464,045    1,972      6.97       4.60
                                          (-log10 p)

F2 BULK-SEGREGANT (xQTL) CROSSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two crosses were performed between strains at opposite extremes of the pooled
assay: N2 x XZ1516 and JU1793 x JU2466. [TO FILL: crossing scheme, F2
population size, selection or competition regime, generations, DNA extraction,
library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Allele counts at parental marker sites were obtained with GATK ASEReadCounter,
summed across sequencing runs, and phased onto the two parental haplotypes at
genetic-map markers. Per-sample allele-frequency deviations and their standard
errors were computed in 10 kb bins, and each pair of conditions was compared as
a contrast, yielding a per-marker effect (contrast.beta), z statistic and LOD.
Deviations are phased to a single parent per cross (N2 in the N2 x XZ1516
cross), so the sign of contrast.beta is interpretable as which parental allele
rose in frequency.

LOD was computed in log space. The p column distributed with these exports is
formed on the linear scale and underflows to exactly zero above |z| ~ 38.5,
which occurs frequently in these data, so LOD (or neglog10p) rather than p was
used for everything quantitative.

The genome-wide significance threshold for the cross scans is LOD 3.57,
obtained as qchisq(2 x 0.05 / 2000, df = 1, lower.tail = FALSE) / (2 ln 10) --
alpha = 0.05 over 2,000 effective tests, in the LOD convention the export
pipeline uses.

Regenerating the exports from the archived per-sample counts reproduces every
peak position and interval bound exactly, with LOD values agreeing to 1e-14
relative (data/cross_experiments/*/README.md).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QTL SUPPORT INTERVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Support intervals were defined as a fractional LOD drop from each chromosome's
own peak marker rather than a fixed number of LOD units, because an absolute
drop wide enough for a strong peak spans an entire chromosome on a weak one.
The shipped interval tables use a 10% drop, calibrated so that a known causal
gene falls inside the interval rather than just outside it. The interval tracks
in Figure 2 use a 5% drop, and are restricted to QTL with a peak LOD above 100
(nine intervals); the complete significant set is
SUPP_FIG_XX_cross_qtl_all. Secondary peaks on the same chromosome were masked
with a 1 Mb buffer either side of a called interval and were required to reach
30% of the chromosome maximum (scripts/Figure2.R).

VARIANT ANNOTATION WITHIN QTL INTERVALS

A encoding 96K. Allele assignment was verified against four strains whose allele
is known independently: JU1793 and N2 carry 96T, JU2466 and XZ1516 carry 96K.

EMBRYO HATCHING ASSAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hatching was scored as the number of plated embryos that failed to hatch on a
lawn of pos-1 RNAi bacteria diluted with HT115. [TO FILL: bacterial strain and
induction, plate preparation, staging of the scored animals, embryo plating
procedure, scoring interval, and who scored blind to genotype.]

The dilution differs between experiments and the numbers are not comparable
across them:

  NIL series (Figure 3C and its supplement)      50% pos-1 RNAi
  JU1793 / JU2466 edits (Figure 4A, supplement)  50% pos-1 RNAi
  N2 edits (Figure 4B)                           25% pos-1 RNAi

The 25% dilution used for the N2 edits is the only one with dynamic range in
that background: across a 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% series, every genotype hatches
at 0% and every genotype is dead from 50% upward, so no other dose can report a
difference (SUPP_FIG_XX_n2_swap_dose). Pooling the two edited lines against N2,
the difference in hatching is +0.282 at 25% (Fisher p = 8e-25) and between
-0.007 and +0.026 at every other dose (p >= 0.11).

IMPORTANT: the dilution is not recorded in the source data files for the NIL
series or the JU1793/JU2466 edits -- their condition column carries only
"pos"/"ht115" -- so those doses come from the lab record. Only n2_swap.tsv
encodes its dose. [TO FILL: confirm both 50% figures, and consider adding a
dose column when those tables are next exported.]

Each strain was assayed on one plate per condition. Reported intervals are
Wilson 95% binomial intervals on that plate's embryo count and therefore
describe counting uncertainty, not between-plate variability; no strain is
replicated. The exception is wSZ203 and wSZ204, which are independently derived
lines carrying the same edit and are the closest approximation to a biological
replicate in these assays. Genotypes were compared with Fisher's exact test on
the hatched/unhatched counts.

PROTEIN TOPOLOGY AND STRUCTURE PREDICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SID-2 topology was predicted with DeepTMHMM, which calls a signal peptide at
residues 1-20, an extracellular region at 21-193, a single transmembrane helix
at 194-211 and a cytoplasmic region at 212-311. The UniProt annotation for
G5EEV9 gives slightly different boundaries (signal 1-20, extracellular 21-188,
transmembrane 189-209, cytoplasmic 210-311); figures state which boundary set
they use.

The SID-2 dimer was predicted with AlphaFold3 (five models). [TO FILL: whether
the AlphaFold Server or a local installation was used, and the date and model
version.] Model confidence limits what the prediction can be used for: ipTM is
0.45-0.46 and pTM 0.46-0.48 across all five models, with 43-46% of the model
called disordered, so the dimer interface is not credible, is not shown in any
figure and dimerisation is not claimed. Mean pLDDT by region for chain A is 56.4
(signal peptide), 72.3 (extracellular), 46.0 (transmembrane helix) and 40.0
(cytoplasmic); only the extracellular domain reaches the confident band, and it
alone is displayed. The variant neighbourhood is locally confident: N94 70.5,
C95 78.0, T96 79.3.

A separate AlphaFold3 run of the trimmed ectodomain with double-stranded RNA
gave better protein-protein confidence (ipTM 0.56-0.58, 7-15% disordered) but
protein-RNA chain-pair ipTM of only 0.22-0.48 across the five models, so the RNA
placement is not reproducible; it was run for the 96K allele only and is not used
in any figure.

Molecular docking of double-stranded RNA was attempted with HADDOCK and is not
reported. Those runs returned positive HADDOCK scores (+98 to +200, where
favourable is negative), interaction energies of order 1e4, restraint-violation
energies of 480-1380 and 4-13 structures per cluster, and the two alleles were
run against different 91-residue ambiguous-restraint sets, so they are not
comparable even in principle.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECONDARY STRUCTURE, ORIENTATION AND DISTANCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Secondary structure was assigned from the model backbone using the Kabsch and
Sander (1983) electrostatic hydrogen-bond criterion, implemented directly
because no DSSP binary was available (scripts/sid2_ribbon_render.py). The amide
hydrogen was placed 1 A from N along the C(i-1)->O(i-1) direction, as DSSP does;
the hydrogen-bond energy was taken as

  E = 332 x 0.42 x 0.20 x (1/r_ON + 1/r_CH - 1/r_OH - 1/r_CN)   kcal/mol

with a bond called at E < -0.5. Alpha helices were assigned from two consecutive
4-turns and strands from residues participating in a parallel or antiparallel
bridge; 3-10 and pi helices were folded into helix, and isolated single-residue
assignments were discarded. The assignment is independently corroborated: it
calls residues 194-211 100% helix, exactly the span DeepTMHMM identifies as the
transmembrane helix, and 60% helix across the signal peptide. The extracellular
domain is beta-rich (46% strand, 3% helix) and T96 lies within a strand, with
N94 in the preceding loop.

For display, the model was used in the membrane frame produced by earlier
orientation work, in which z = 0 is the bilayer centre and +z is the intestinal
lumen (tilt 32.5 degrees to the membrane normal, hydrophobic thickness 32.4 A,
transmembrane helix spanning z = -13.2 to +13.2 A). Residue 96 sits at z = +62 A,
on the lumenal face.

Distances between residue 96 and the residues with a published effect on dsRNA
uptake were measured C-alpha to C-alpha: D34 13.5 A, H32 16.4 A,
H168 19.2 A and H175 37.7 A, against a median C-alpha distance from residue 96
of 23.8 A across the ectodomain. Nearest heavy-atom distances are shorter (D34
9.9, H32 14.6, H168 16.9 A). Because 41% of the ectodomain lies within 20 A of
residue 96, three of the four annotated residues falling within that radius is
not surprising: the binomial probability is 0.19, and a permutation test on
their mean distance against 20,000 random sets of four ectodomain residues gives
p = 0.30. The co-localisation is therefore reported as spatial context and not
as evidence of a shared site.

[Note: D34 is 13.5 A from residue 96 in the membrane-oriented ectodomain file
and 13.8 A in the full-length AlphaFold model, because the two files are
different exports of the same prediction; figures state which file they use.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ECTODOMAIN CHARGE AS A FUNCTION OF pH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Net charge of the extracellular domain (residues 21-193: 11 Asp, 7 Glu, 3 His,
4 Cys, 5 Tyr, 9 Lys, 1 Arg) was computed by Henderson-Hasselbalch over the side
chains with pKa values Asp 3.90, Glu 4.07, His 6.04, Cys 8.18, Tyr 10.46, Lys
10.54 and Arg 12.48. Termini were omitted, because the ectodomain is not a free
polypeptide -- it continues into the transmembrane helix -- so a free
alpha-carboxylate would be an artefact of where the domain was cut.

The domain carries -8.4 e at pH 7.4 but only -0.2 e at pH 4.4, and its
isoelectric point, 4.38, is close to the pH of the intestinal lumen. Adding the
+1 e of T96K takes the surface from -0.2 e to +0.8 e at pH 4.4 and moves the
isoelectric point to 4.50. This is presented as a hypothesis-framing
calculation, not as an estimate of binding: no affinity, free energy or
occupancy is computed anywhere in this work.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STATISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Binomial proportions are reported with Wilson score 95% intervals, which behave
correctly near 0 and 1 where the Wald interval does not. Counts were compared
with Fisher's exact test. Rank correlations are Spearman's rho; the plate
phenotype score is a six-level ordinal scale, so rank correlation is used for
every comparison involving it. Two-group comparisons of continuous phenotypes
use the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with Welch's t test reported alongside where the
two disagree, and both are reported rather than the more favourable one.
Permutation tests are stated with their number of draws. No correction for
multiple comparisons was applied within the small hatching experiments; the
genome-wide thresholds are given above.
