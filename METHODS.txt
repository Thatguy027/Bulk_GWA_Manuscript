POOLED RNAi COMPETITION EXPERIMENT AND PHENOTYPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A pool of wild isolates was grown on bacteria expressing double-stranded RNA
against each of ten target genes (fog-2, mig-6, pos-1, rde-3, ric-3, rpn-12,
spe-19, spe-43, unc-39, vha-5) and on the HT115 empty-vector control, and the
pool was sequenced at two timepoints. [TO FILL: pool composition and how it was
assembled, generations of competition, timepoint definitions, replicate
structure, DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Per-strain phenotypes were derived from the timepoint-2 frequencies and are
distributed in data/pooled_RNAi_expt/reanalysis/vst_association_traits.csv in
three parameterisations per target: delta_ctrl_<gene>_T2, the RNAi-minus-control
change in pool frequency; log2fc_<gene>_T2, its log2 ratio; and
vst_<gene>_T2, a variance-stabilised transform. All association mapping and all
phenotype figures in this manuscript use the vst columns, so the Manhattan
panels and the phenotype panels are on the same scale.

Nine of the 93 strains in the pool have no vst value and were dropped, leaving
n = 84 for association mapping.

The 2023 pos-1 experiment was analysed separately; its full analysis lives
outside this repository at /UCLA/Projects/bulkGWAS/lipid_RNAi/2023_pos1 and only
its exported end products are used here
(data/pos1_original/updated_analysis/). Sample-level frequencies are
distributed at three read-depth cutoffs; cutoff 5 was used throughout, because
taking the mean pos-1 delta per strain at cutoff 5 reproduces the shipped
delta_ctrl_pos-1_T2 to 2.5e-16, against r = 0.9998 at cutoff 3 and r = 0.9988 at
cutoff 10 (scripts/2023_pos1_analysis.R).

One strain, JU1793, appears twice per sample in that dataset at every depth
cutoff and in both arms, with one row carrying the frequency and the other
approximately 1e-21 -- non-negative least squares splitting one strain's
abundance across two identical entries in the genotype reference. Rows sharing
a (strain, sample) key were therefore summed before deltas were taken, which
approximately doubles JU1793's pos-1 delta, from 0.00857 to 0.0171, and is a
no-op for every other strain. This matters because JU1793 is a cross parent.
The shipped vst values and the association scan were used as distributed and
still carry the averaged JU1793 value.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STRAIN-FREQUENCY DECONVOLUTION FROM POOLED SEQUENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Strain frequencies were recovered from pooled sequence by non-negative least
squares against a genotype reference matrix. Markers with any missing genotype
call were dropped rather than zero-filled, because a zero is a real
homozygous-reference call in this encoding and imputing one would alter the
design matrix (in the Baugh reference matrix used for validation, all 1,237,106
markers are complete, so the filter is a no-op there). Writing G for the
genotype matrix and a for the per-sample allele counts, the normal-equation
inputs G'a and G'G were formed and solved per sample with RcppML::nnls, and each
sample's solution was normalised to sum to one
(scripts/Figure1_common.R, baugh_frequencies()).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VALIDATION OF THE DECONVOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The deconvolution was validated three times, each holding a different quantity
known: in simulation, where the input frequencies and the counts are both
synthetic; on a designed DNA mixture, where the input is known and the counts
are real; and against a published independent measurement of the same samples,
where neither is controlled. They are reported in that order.

Simulation. To determine whether non-negative least squares (NNLS) regression
can infer strain frequencies from pooled populations, we established a
simulation framework. Each wild isolate was assigned a fitness value taken from
one of seven published C. elegans traits with validated QTL
(Albendazole_q75.TOF, PC1, assay_norm, mtDNA_ratio, value, amsacrine_f.L1 and
etoposide_median.TOF), giving seven independent simulated populations of 327
strains. The trait value itself was the fitness, shifted by the trait's own
minimum so that fitness is non-negative; a strain with no published value for a
trait was assigned fitness zero and so was absent from that trait's pool. The
expected pooled allele frequencies such a population would produce were
computed from the CeNDR genotype matrix, and observed alt-allele counts were
simulated by binomial sampling at 1, 3, 5, 10, 30, 50, 100 and 500x. Those
counts were deconvolved back to per-strain frequencies by NNLS and compared
with the known input. Recovery was quantified as the r-squared of estimated
against known input frequency at each depth.

The simulation is run from source with a seed, in ten independent replicates
(scripts/make_simulation_seeded.R, seed 20260909; deposited as
simulation_seeded_frequencies.tsv.gz and simulation_seeded_r2.tsv), and the
figure reports the mean over replicates with a band spanning them
(scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_simulation_depth.R). Replication is not decoration here:
the single 2021 draw reported each recovery as one exact number, and the depth
at which the worst trait crosses a threshold turns out not to be stable to
resampling.

At 1x, recovery ranges from 0.91 (PC1) to 0.51 (mtDNA_ratio), median 0.76, and
the replicate bands are widest at that depth -- up to 0.149 for assay_norm.
Counting a trait as clearing r-squared 0.95 only where it does so in every
replicate, 5 of the 7 traits clear it at 10x, SIX OF SEVEN CLEAR IT BY 30x, AND
50x CLEARS ALL SEVEN, where the lowest r-squared anywhere in the ten replicates
is 0.9639. The exception at 30x is mtDNA_ratio, which sits on the line rather
than below it: it averages 0.9504 across the replicates, spans 0.9443 to
0.9610, and clears the bar in four of the ten. No single depth is therefore
quoted for all seven traits. Earlier drafts quoted one -- first 30x, then 50x --
and the difference between those two answers is a draw, not a measurement. No
trait reaches r-squared of 1.000 even at 500x, where the range is 0.9966
(mtDNA_ratio) to 0.9998 (PC1); an earlier draft said all seven reached 1.00 by
500x, which came from reading values rounded to two decimals.

The 2021 run is retained rather than discarded. Its estimates remain deposited,
all 56 of its r-squared are recomputable exactly from them
(scripts/simulation_recompute_r2.R), and its values fall inside the
ten-replicate band in 43 of 56 trait-depth cells, so it is consistent with the
replicated run rather than anomalous. Two properties of it are what motivated
regenerating: its binomial draw was never seeded, so it could be recomputed
against but never reproduced, and it carried 139 of 18,312 negative
coefficients (0.8%) that a strict non-negative solver cannot return. The seeded
run carries none, and the figure script asserts that.

The simulation code has since been recovered and is archived as
scripts/simulation_deconvolution.R, a port of the original working script kept
verbatim at scripts/legacy/haploReg_original.R. The parameters it pins:

  Fitness was drawn as extraDistr::rinvchisq(n, df = 12, scale = 1). Expected
  pooled allele frequency was the fitness-weighted mean over the strains
  carrying each alternate allele -- rowSums(G %*% w) / sum(w), with G the
  genotype matrix recoded to a 0/1 indicator of the alternate allele. The
  design matrix passed to NNLS was G stacked on its own complement,
  rbind(G, 1 - G), so that reference-allele counts entered as their own
  equations; in simulation this is worth about 0.14 of r-squared at 1x and
  0.08 at 10x over the unstacked design.

The inverse chi-squared draw pinned above belongs to the exploratory arm of the
original script, not to the seven-trait simulation whose r-squared are quoted
here. The seven traits, and the strains carrying each, are deposited as
supplemental_data/deconvolution/simulation_fitness_traits.tsv.

This is established by reproduction rather than by reading the code. Given
those trait values, all 56 r-squared -- seven traits at eight depths --
recompute from the archived NNLS estimates and reproduce the values previously
transcribed from text embedded in the original per-trait figures exactly at the
two decimals those figures carry (scripts/simulation_recompute_r2.R, checked on
every push; the figure script asserts the same thing). The r-squared are
therefore computed rather than transcribed, and both panels of
SUPP_FIG_XX_simulation_depth are now against the known input;
scripts/extract_sim_reported_r2.py remains only as the record of where the
values were read from before the inputs were recovered.

One caveat the recomputation makes explicit: the r-squared are computed over
all 327 strains with the unmeasured ones held at exactly zero, which flatters
the correlation. The number of strains actually carrying a published value
ranges from 327 for mtDNA_ratio down to 84 for PC1, and restricted to those
strains the pooled r-squared falls from 0.79 to 0.70 at 1x and from 0.97 to
0.95 at 10x.

The binomial draw over expected frequencies was not seeded -- the three
set.seed calls in the original belong to its bootstrap, panel-size and dropout
arms -- so the particular simulated counts behind the archived estimates cannot
be regenerated. This does not affect the r-squared above, which are computed
from the archived estimates themselves, but it does mean a fresh run redraws
rather than reproduces. How much that costs was measured rather than assumed.

The seven-trait simulation was re-run from the CeNDR 20210121 panel
(2,917,997 markers x 327 strains), three independent replicates at all eight
depths, with three estimators applied to identical simulated counts so that
redraw noise and estimator choice could be separated: mcrals.fcnnls on the raw
design, which is the archived run's own call; mcrals.fcnnls on the normal
equations; and RcppML::nnls on the normal equations, which is what
scripts/simulation_deconvolution.R does.

  The estimator is immaterial. The archived call and the port agree to a median
  of 4e-06 and a maximum of 1.1e-03 in r-squared. The formulation is what
  matters: mcrals.fcnnls given the normal equations rather than the raw design
  disagrees by up to 6.2e-02 and reports hitting its iteration cap, because
  forming the cross-product matrix squares the condition number.

  The unseeded draw costs about 0.01 of r-squared at 10x and less at greater
  depth. The archived values deviate from the redraw mean by a median of
  -0.0002, over a range of -0.0245 to +0.0808, and fall inside the range of
  three replicates in 32 of 56 trait-depth cells. At 50x and 100x they usually
  fall outside it -- not because the deviation is large, but because the redraw
  scatter there is only 0.001 to 0.002 and a systematic offset of about 0.0004
  survives.

The reported r-squared are therefore reproducible in substance: a fresh
simulation returns the same recovery curves to within about 0.01, and every
value in the archive is recomputable exactly from the archived estimates. They
are not demonstrably drawn from the same distribution as a redraw, because a
small residual offset persists at high depth that three replicates cannot
attribute.

Designed DNA mixture. 174 wild isolates were divided into four sets of roughly
equal size (A, B, C, D). Genomic DNA from each set was pooled and sequenced both
pure -- three libraries per set -- and as a seven-step titration of set B
against set C. Alt-allele counts were taken with GATK ASEReadCounter, markers
were restricted to those with mean depth across samples above 5 and below 100,
high-frequency variants were recoded so that the minor allele is counted
throughout, sites with any missing genotype were dropped, and the resulting
counts were deconvolved by NNLS (scripts/legacy/dilution_experiment.R;
scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_dilution_validation.R). Pure pools returned 0.775 to 0.853
of their own set, with 0.020 to 0.127 misassigned to each other set. Across the
titration, set B rose monotonically from 0.12 to 0.72 and set C fell
monotonically from 0.70 to 0.10 (Spearman rho +1 and -1 against titration step),
while the two untitrated sets stayed flat. Restricting the genotype reference to
the 84 strains of sets B and C and renormalising gives complementary shares
running from 0.17:0.83 to 0.84:0.16.

The titration was a two-fold doubling series of set B against a fixed 1 uL of
set C, made up to 10 uL with water, giving designed B fractions of 0.091, 0.167,
0.286, 0.444, 0.616, 0.762 and 0.865. Stock concentrations were measured at
100 ng/uL for B1 and 99.9 ng/uL for C1, so the DNA mass fraction equals the
volume fraction to within 2.5e-4. Total input DNA was not held constant, rising
from 11 ng at BC1 to 74 ng at BC7, because only the B volume was varied; input
mass and B fraction are consequently confounded across the series. Each dilution
was prepared once, so pipetting error is unreplicated.

Recovered set fractions tracked the design with Pearson r = 0.997 and a root
mean squared error of 0.038 in fraction units (Spearman rho = 1). The largest
single deviation was at the first step (+0.080), whose 0.1 uL of set B is the
smallest volume in the series; excluding it gives RMSE 0.024. Recovery was
biased toward set B by a mean of 0.021, which stock concentration does not
explain and which is not accounted for here.

Per-strain resolution was related to genetic similarity as follows. A strain
absent from a pool must be assigned a frequency of zero in that pool's
libraries whatever the DNA input was, so for each strain the mean frequency
assigned across the nine libraries of the three sets it does not belong to was
taken as an assumption-free error measure ("leakage"). Genetic similarity was
summarised as identity-by-state -- the fraction of markers at which two strains
carry the same allele -- to the closest other strain in the reference, computed
over 231,615 variable markers sampled under a fixed seed from the CeNDR
genotype matrix. Leakage rises with nearest-neighbour identity (Spearman
rho = 0.326, p = 1.4e-05, n = 170), while the fraction of its own pool a strain
recovers does not (rho = 0.046, p = 0.55). Genetic similarity therefore predicts
misassignment into pools a strain is absent from, without predicting how well
that strain's own share is recovered.

One isotype is assigned to two sets in this experiment: strains JU1580 and
JU1793 share the isotype JU1793 and were placed in sets B and D respectively.
Because the deconvolution operates at isotype level and returns a single
frequency for the pair, that estimate is attributable to either set; it is
counted in set B throughout, which is both what the original analysis did and
what the estimate's behaviour across the titration indicates.

Accuracy was assessed against published molecular inversion probe sequencing
(MIP-seq) frequencies for the Baugh L1 starvation time course (Webster et al.
2022, eLife 11:e80204), the one dataset in which both measurements exist for
the same samples (23 samples).

Three strain counts appear in this comparison and they are not interchangeable.
The deconvolution reference carries 102 strains; MIP-seq frequencies were
published for 100; 99 are common to both. Excluding N2, for the reason given
below, leaves the n = 98 on which the correlations in this section are
computed. The three reference strains with no MIP-seq measurement are CX11262,
ECA348 and NIC260, and PB306 has a MIP-seq measurement but is absent from the
reference.

For each strain and replicate arm, the rate of change in pool frequency was
taken as the slope of the regression of frequency change on day, with day 1 as
the baseline and day 17 excluded, and slopes were averaged over the five
replicate arms. Slopes were computed in closed form
(sum((x - xbar) y) / sum((x - xbar)^2)), which is identical to the least-squares
coefficient.

BOTH PLATFORMS WERE PUT THROUGH ONE IDENTICAL TRANSFORMATION, which is what
makes the correlation a comparison of measurements rather than of methods. In
scripts/Figure1_common.R the function platform_slopes() takes one frame, applies
the same day-1 baseline and the same day-17 exclusion to both columns, forms
both differences in a single step, and passes each through the same slope
function grouped the same way. The only asymmetry between the two is which
column is read.

That slope is NOT on the scale of the Slope trait published by Webster et al.
Theirs is fitted to log2-transformed frequencies normalised to day 1 with the
intercept forced through the origin; the slope here is a raw difference from
day 1 with a free intercept, x being centred before the closed-form fit. Day 1
as the baseline, day 13 as the last point and the exclusion of day 17 agree
between the two. The rank correlation is unaffected -- it compares two columns
treated identically -- but the slope VALUES should not be compared numerically
against the published trait. N2 was excluded from all comparisons: as the reference strain its
genotype shares sites with every other strain in the pool, so its frequency is
not identified on the same footing as the wild isolates'. Agreement was
quantified by Spearman correlation of the two platforms' slopes across strains
(n = 98, rho = 0.97, p < 1e-4) and, separately, per sample across strains
(23 samples, median rho = 0.84, minimum 0.517).

Uncertainty on the slopes was obtained by repeating the entire slope
calculation inside each of 100 bootstrap replicates of the deconvolution --
delta against the day-1 sample of the same replicate arm, regression on day,
average over arms -- rather than by propagating a stored standard error through
that chain, and the reported interval is the percentile interval across
replicates. Each replicate supplies its own baseline, so the covariance between
baseline and later timepoints is preserved. The bootstrap distribution of the
Spearman correlation itself is rho = 0.972, 95% interval [0.969, 0.975]. That
the array resamples the plotted estimator, and that the transformation is
carried through it consistently, was verified at four points
(scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_bootstrap_propagation_checks.R): bootstrap mean against
point estimate at the frequency level (r = 0.999984, n = 2,346) and at the slope
level (r = 0.999994, n = 101); a standardised bias of median -0.029 bootstrap
SD; and coverage of 101 of 101 intervals containing their own point estimate.

The bootstrap resampled MARKERS with replacement, 100 replicates at seed 100,
refitting the deconvolution from scratch within each replicate. This is now
recoverable: the resampling loop is in the working script archived as
scripts/legacy/haploReg_original.R and ported to
scripts/simulation_deconvolution.R.

That the unit is markers rather than reads or strains is fixed by the shape of
the archived output. baugh_bootstrap_array.rda carries objects named ab, blist
and bootse -- the original script's own variable names -- with ab dimensioned
102 x 23 x 100, that is strains x samples x replicates. Resampling rows of a
markers x strains matrix is the only one of the three candidates that yields a
strains x samples estimate per replicate; resampling strains would change the
first dimension, and reads do not index the matrix at all.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLE-TESTING THRESHOLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two thresholds are reported on every association panel. The first is Bonferroni
over every tested marker, which treats markers as independent. They are not:
these panels carry long haplotype blocks, so that threshold is
over-conservative. The second divides alpha = 0.05 by the effective number of
independent tests estimated from the eigenvalues of the marker correlation
matrix by the method of Li and Ji (2005), in which each eigenvalue lambda
contributes 1{lambda >= 1} + (lambda - floor(lambda)).

Computing the M x M marker correlation matrix directly is infeasible at these
marker counts. Instead the non-zero eigenvalues of that matrix were obtained
from the n x n matrix A = Zc Zc' / (n - 1), where Zc is the column-standardised
genotype matrix; the two matrices share their non-zero spectrum. Missing calls
were mean-imputed per marker rather than dropped, because dropping any marker
with a missing call removed about 40% of markers and silently changed the
denominator; the Bonferroni threshold is computed over the same marker set.
trace(A) = M was asserted at run time as a correctness check
(scripts/eigen_independent_tests.R; accessor in scripts/gwas_thresholds.R).

  panel              markers    M_eff   Bonferroni   Li & Ji
  pooled_RNAi_expt   322,010      732      6.81       4.17
  pos1_2023          464,045    1,972      6.97       4.60
                                          (-log10 p)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F2 BULK-SEGREGANT (xQTL) CROSSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two crosses were performed between strains at opposite extremes of the pooled
assay: N2 x XZ1516 and JU1793 x JU2466. [TO FILL: crossing scheme, F2
population size, selection or competition regime, generations, DNA extraction,
library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Allele counts at parental marker sites were obtained with GATK ASEReadCounter,
summed across sequencing runs, and phased onto the two parental haplotypes at
genetic-map markers. Per-sample allele-frequency deviations and their standard
errors were computed in 10 kb bins, and each pair of conditions was compared as
a contrast, yielding a per-marker effect (contrast.beta), z statistic and LOD.
Deviations are phased to a single parent per cross (N2 in the N2 x XZ1516
cross), so the sign of contrast.beta is interpretable as which parental allele
rose in frequency.

LOD was computed in log space. The p column distributed with these exports is
formed on the linear scale and underflows to exactly zero above |z| ~ 38.5,
which occurs frequently in these data, so LOD (or neglog10p) rather than p was
used for everything quantitative.

The genome-wide significance threshold for the cross scans is LOD 3.57,
obtained as qchisq(2 x 0.05 / 2000, df = 1, lower.tail = FALSE) / (2 ln 10) --
alpha = 0.05 over 2,000 effective tests, in the LOD convention the export
pipeline uses.

Regenerating the exports from the archived per-sample counts reproduces every
peak position and interval bound exactly, with LOD values agreeing to 1e-14
relative (data/cross_experiments/*/README.md).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QTL SUPPORT INTERVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Support intervals were defined as a fractional LOD drop from each chromosome's
own peak marker rather than a fixed number of LOD units, because an absolute
drop wide enough for a strong peak spans an entire chromosome on a weak one.
The shipped interval tables use a 10% drop, calibrated so that a known causal
gene falls inside the interval rather than just outside it. The interval tracks
in Figure 2 use a 5% drop, and are restricted to QTL with a peak LOD above 100
(nine intervals); the complete significant set is
SUPP_FIG_XX_cross_qtl_all. Secondary peaks on the same chromosome were masked
with a 1 Mb buffer either side of a called interval and were required to reach
30% of the chromosome maximum (scripts/Figure2.R).

Because those display constants do more filtering than the significance
threshold does, a complete peak table was built separately
(scripts/cross_qtl_full_summary.R): every peak above the genome-wide threshold
in every contrast, with its interval, its parental frequencies and its rank on
its chromosome. Secondary peaks were admitted as independent QTL only if the
LOD trough between them and the taller peak on the same chromosome falls below
the genome-wide threshold; a secondary peak with no such trough is a shoulder of
one sweep rather than a separate locus, and is reported but flagged. Of 495
peaks above threshold, 46 are drawn in Figure 2 and 387 are secondary peaks that
Figure 2's one-peak-per-chromosome constant cannot call.

Three contrasts were run in both crosses, so a QTL can be asked whether the
other cross calls it too. Two criteria are reported rather than one. The
stricter is interval overlap: the two crosses' LOD-drop intervals intersect.
The weaker is positional, that the other cross's scan clears the genome-wide
threshold at this peak's own position. Overlap alone is too strict at these
depths, because pooled LOD runs into the hundreds and collapses the drop
intervals -- JU1793 x JU2466's pos-1 peak on chromosome III has one 50 kb wide
-- so a peak offset of a few hundred kb defeats it even where both crosses are
unambiguous at each other's peak. On interval overlap alone 19 QTL are shared;
adding positional matches brings it to 45. The sid-2 region is the case that
decides it: matched by interval for HT115 vs mig-6 in both directions, and only
positionally for HT115 vs pos-1, where the two peaks sit 469 kb apart with
0.39 Mb and 0.05 Mb intervals that miss by 234 kb while the two crosses reach
LOD 710 and 140 at their own peaks and LOD 88 and 522 at each other's.

GENERAL AGAINST TARGET-SPECIFIC RESPONSE LOCI. N2 x XZ1516 ran five knockdown
pools against one shared HT115 control, so a locus can be classified by how
many targets respond at it rather than inferred from a flat difference trace;
the JU cross ran two, which is its ceiling and is marked as such
(scripts/cross_qtl_condition_sharing.R). Classification is on effect size with
significance necessary only, because at these depths nearly every target is
significant nearly everywhere: on significance alone 55 of 61 N2 x XZ1516 loci
came out general, 19 of them in all five targets, which is not credible. A
target counts as responding at a locus when its pool differs from HT115 by
|dfreq| >= 0.10 with LOD above the genome-wide threshold (3.57), and a locus is
general when at least three targets respond IN THE SAME DIRECTION. Requiring
comparable magnitude instead is the wrong test -- mig-6 shifts about three
times as far as par-1 at the chromosome V loci -- and called nothing general at
all. Two facts constrain the interpretation and are reported with the counts:
every contrast within a cross shares one HT115 pool, so drift in that pool
imitates a general locus, and the timepoint-1 replicate shows that replicate
agreement of the frequency shift depends sharply on the target (mig-6 r = 0.975,
rpn-12 r = 0.683, par-1 r = 0.125), so par-1 significance is not by itself
evidence of a response. The general set is sensitive to where the response cut
is placed (29, 7, 2 and 1 loci at |dfreq| >= 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20) while
mig-6-specific calls are stable (13, 17, 18, 16); both sweeps are reported.

VARIANT ANNOTATION WITHIN QTL INTERVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Variants differing between the parents of each cross were extracted within every
support interval and annotated for consequence with bcftools csq (BCSQ)
(scripts/pooled_cross_candidate_variation.R), giving 62,988 parental-difference
records across 36 intervals. Per-interval tables are distributed under
plots/pooled_cross_intersection/. Within the resolved chromosome III interval,
sid-2 carries two protein-altering differences between JU1793 and JU2466: V5L,
in the predicted signal peptide, and T96K.

The T96K site is chromosome III 13,680,248 C>A, with REF C encoding 96T and ALT
A encoding 96K. Allele assignment was verified against four strains whose allele
is known independently: JU1793 and N2 carry 96T, JU2466 and XZ1516 carry 96K.

A THIRD, INCOMPLETE CROSS: JU2466 x XZ1516. Both parents carry sid-2 96K, so
T96K cannot segregate between them, which makes this the only cross that can
separate the T96K story from anything else on the chromosome III right arm. It
is reported as a diagnostic rather than a result because it is incomplete:
there is no HT115 control pool, so every contrast is one RNAi condition against
another and no contrast measures a response against an unselected baseline;
only three timepoint-2 pools exist (pos-1, mig-6, par-1). Data are staged from
the external analysis directory by scripts/make_jx_cross_chr3_tables.R and
analysed by scripts/diagnostic_xz1516_sid2.R.

The cross does carry a chromosome III right-arm QTL. From raw counts in a
50 kb window on sid-2 the JU2466 allele runs 0.844 in the pos-1 pool, 0.775 in
mig-6 and 0.573 in par-1, so the XZ1516 haplotype is the one removed wherever
RNAi selects; two independent RNAi conditions agree, which argues against a
par-1 artefact, but par-1 at 0.573 rather than 0.50 cannot be interpreted
without the missing control.

Two explanations were tested. First, an allelic series at sid-2: XZ1516 carries
five missense variants JU2466 does not (D78A, M141V, Q144P, A151I, L209M) plus
a splice-region variant, against only 96K and P153T shared. Figure 4's
mechanism for T96K is electrostatic, and on that mechanism the series does not
work: only D78A can change formal charge at all, the other four being
neutral-to-neutral substitutions, and D78A lies 35.1 A from T96 -- the furthest
of the five -- in a locally negative environment (local net charge -1.28)
rather than in T96's positive pocket. The splice-region variant is untested and
remains the one sid-2 difference this analysis cannot exclude.

Second, the other chromosome III locus. The mig-6 census identified an
independent general-response locus at III:13.31 Mb (interval 13.090-13.526,
general:4 in N2 x XZ1516), 375 kb proximal to sid-2. Two of the three contrasts
in this cross peak inside that interval and closer to it than to sid-2 --
pos-1 minus par-1 at 13.365 Mb with LOD 145.5 and LOD 145.1 at 13.31, and
pos-1 minus mig-6 at 13.286 Mb -- while the third, mig-6 minus par-1, peaks at
13.784 Mb with a higher LOD at sid-2 than at 13.31. The reading taken is
therefore that two independent crosses converge on the same non-sid-2 locus at
13.31 Mb rather than that sid-2 carries an allelic series, without excluding
sid-2, whose position falls inside the broad pos-1 interval at LOD 134.8.

SID-2 ACROSS SPECIES. C. briggsae is insensitive to environmental RNAi and a
C. elegans sid-2 transgene confers sensitivity on it (Winston et al. 2007), so
the two species' SID-2 proteins were aligned to ask which residues a second
species conserves (scripts/make_sid2_alignment_tables.py, drawn as
SUPP_FIG_XX_sid2_briggsae_alignment). Sequences are UniProt G5EEV9
(C. elegans SID-2, 311 aa) and A8XSB8 (C. briggsae CBR-SID-2, CBG18280,
314 aa); the C. briggsae entry is unreviewed and its gene model was not checked
against C. briggsae RNA-seq. Alignment is global Needleman-Wunsch with BLOSUM62
and gap penalties -11 open, -1 extend. The two proteins are 47.3% identical
over 296 aligned pairs (140 identical, 156 different, 15 gapped), so the
alignment is a hypothesis about residue correspondence rather than a fact, and
the staged per-position table records the aligned C. briggsae residue and a gap
flag so that the weak regions are visible. Residues 32, 34, 94, 96, 168 and 175
all fall in well-aligned blocks. Two species do not support a conservation
analysis; the ortholog survey described below supplies one.

All three histidines implicated in dsRNA uptake differ in C. briggsae -- H32 to
arginine, H168 to serine, H175 to arginine -- while N94, T96 and D34 are
identical. Those histidines are described as implicated and not as critical:
they are the residues McEwan et al. 2012 mutated, and that paper's triple
His-to-Arg mutant internalised MORE dsRNA than wild type, so the C. briggsae
state at two of them, which is that same arginine, cannot by itself explain why
C. briggsae does not take dsRNA up.

SID-2 ORTHOLOG SURVEY ACROSS NEMATODES. Because sid-2 96K is a polymorphism
within C. elegans, calling 96T ancestral requires outgroups. C. elegans SID-2
(UniProt G5EEV9) was searched by reciprocal-best-hit blastp against 20 UniProt
reference proteomes spanning Caenorhabditis and the major nematode clades,
488,718 proteins in total: C. briggsae UP000008549, C. nigoni UP000230233,
C. tropicalis UP000095282, C. remanei UP000008281, C. latens UP000216463,
C. brenneri UP000008068, C. japonica UP000005237, C. bovis UP000494206,
C. auriculariae UP000835052, C. angaria UP001152747, Diploscapter pachys
UP000218231, Pristionchus mayeri UP001328107, Heterorhabditis bacteriophora
UP000095283, Haemonchus contortus UP000025227, Panagrellus redivivus
UP000492821, Strongyloides ratti UP000035682, Bursaphelenchus xylophilus
UP000659654, Brugia malayi UP000006672, Trichinella spiralis UP000054776, and
C. elegans UP000001940 itself as a control. One BLAST database was built per
proteome and searched with NCBI blastp, -evalue 10 -seg no -max_target_seqs 5;
forward best hits were confirmed by searching the hit back against the
C. elegans proteome. A hit was called an ortholog at E < 1e-5.

Eight of the 19 UniProt comparator proteomes yielded an ortholog and every one
of them is a Caenorhabditis. No proteome outside the genus cleared the
threshold, including Diploscapter pachys, the sister genus, whose best hit was
E = 4.1. Two independent resources agree: the UniRef50 cluster containing
G5EEV9 has one member, and NCBI's ortholog set for sid-2 within Nematoda is
empty. Adding the Caenorhabditis Genomes Project proteomes below takes the
survey to 51 comparators, of which 38 yield an ortholog and every one is still
a Caenorhabditis, so widening the search did not move the genus boundary
outward; it filled in the inside of it, and the bound now rests on 42
Caenorhabditis comparator proteomes rather than 10. Four Caenorhabditis
proteomes fail: C. bovis (E = 2.2), C. monodelphis (no hit) and
C. auriculariae in both of its independent proteomes (UniProt E = 0.15, CGP no
hit). Absence of a hit in a single proteome is weak evidence about the gene and
may reflect incomplete annotation, so no gene loss is claimed even for the
species that fails twice.

Per-position conservation was computed over the six Elegans-group orthologs by
global Needleman-Wunsch alignment of each to C. elegans SID-2 with BLOSUM62 and
gap open -11, extend -1 -- the same settings as the two-species alignment above
-- and counting, at each C. elegans position, how many orthologs carry the
C. elegans residue. Across the 173 ectodomain positions the mean is 2.23 of 6
(37.1%) and 15.0% of positions are conserved in all six. The N94-C95-T96 sequon
separates sharply against that background: N94 is conserved in 6 of 6 (92nd
percentile of ectodomain positions), C95 in 0 of 6 (16th), and T96 in 5 of 6 and
in 6 of 6 as serine or threonine (82nd). The contrast between the sequon's two
constrained positions and its unconstrained middle comes from the same alignment
columns, which is why it is reported in preference to the conservation of
residue 96 alone: threonine is 13.3% of this ectodomain, so a single conserved
threonine in a 42-48% identity alignment would be weak evidence.

Three of the five ectodomain sequons (71, 81, 94) are intact in all six
orthologs and two (100, 148) in one, so a fully conserved sequon is the norm in
this protein rather than a distinction.

WIDENING THE SPECIES SET WITH THE CAENORHABDITIS GENOMES PROJECT. UniProt
carries only a handful of Caenorhabditis proteomes, so the survey was extended
with the v2 release of the Caenorhabditis Genomes Project (caenorhabditis.org,
archived at doi:10.5281/zenodo.12633738), which adds 32 proteomes and 750,673
proteins. Only one of the 32, C. auriculariae, is a species the UniProt set
already carried, so the two sets together are 52 proteomes covering 51 species,
1,239,391 proteins. These were searched identically. For the wider set the
residues aligned to C. elegans
94-96 were read from the BLAST high-scoring pair that spans those positions
rather than from a global alignment, because a global alignment of the full
311-residue query against a partially covered ortholog misplaces the window.
Each call carries a confidence, the local identity of the +/-10 residue block
around the window; the floor for counting a call is 37%, which is the level the
six full-length Elegans-group orthologs already occupied (they span 37-47%)
rather than a threshold chosen to produce a result. Species whose window falls
below the floor are reported and counted neither way.

Across the 14 species at or above the floor, residue 96 is serine or threonine
in 12 -- eleven threonine, one serine -- and lysine in none, and the N94-x-[ST]
sequon is intact in 11. Of the three losses, only C. doughertyi substitutes
residue 96 itself, to alanine; C. afra retains Thr96 and loses Asn94, and
C. sp54 changes both. The constraint therefore sits on residue 96 being small
and hydroxylated rather than on the sequon as a unit, and C. afra is a naturally
occurring equivalent of the N94A construct, which the editing series shows to be
fully resistant. Two species below the floor appear to carry lysine at 96
(C. sp44, C. sp27) but their block identities are 32% and 21%, so those calls
are not used.

A FIRST PASS OVERSTATED THE WITHIN-GENUS BREAKDOWN, and the correction is
recorded here because the earlier reading reached a draft. With the UniProt
species alone it appeared that the sequon was intact through the Elegans group
and lost at C. angaria, the most distant species with a detectable ortholog.
The C. angaria call came from a global alignment whose block identity around
the window is 26%, too low to read a column from, and the UniProt sample
contained no Elegans-group species lacking the sequon, which the wider sample
shows do exist. C. japonica likewise cannot be scored: its ortholog is split
across two proteome entries (A0A8R1DPT1 covering C. elegans 167-311 and
A0A8R1IAX9 covering 21-127) and within the N-terminal fragment the alignment
falls in a TTDT repeat offset by two to three residues.

ENVIRONMENTAL RNAi COMPETENCE, LAYERED ON FROM THE LITERATURE. Species were
annotated with their published response to ingested double-stranded RNA from
Nuez & Felix 2012 (PLoS ONE 7:e29811), which scored Caenorhabditis species for
the response to ingested Oti-actin dsRNA. That paper designates species by
provisional numbers, most of which have since been named, so every call was
mapped through Felix, Braendle & Cutter 2014 (PLoS ONE 9:e94723), whose type
strains are the isolates Nuez & Felix tested; the provisional numbering is
Kiontke et al. 2011 (BMC Evol Biol 11:339). The mapping is deposited rather than
applied silently, because the correspondences are not obvious: C. sp. 11 is
C. tropicalis and C. sp. 10 is C. doughertyi. Only calls that paper states in
prose were used. Its Table S1 is a workbook of per-species Wilcoxon tests rather
than a table of calls, and scoring from it would require deciding which p value
belongs to the test strain and which to the N2 control on each sheet; species
the paper tested but does not call in prose are therefore absent from the layer
rather than inferred, C. doughertyi and C. nigoni among them. C. tropicalis is
included because its own sheet gives p = 0.93 and the paper's Table S2 exists to
report the attempt to rescue it.

For most species the isolate scored for RNAi is not the isolate whose genome was
sequenced. Only C. elegans (N2), C. tropicalis (JU1373) and C. drosophilae
(DF5077) match; the sequenced strains were resolved through each UniProt
proteome's assembly accession and the corresponding NCBI dataset report. This is
recorded per row because the same paper reports intraspecific variation in
C. elegans, so a species-level phenotype cannot be assumed to hold for a
different isolate.

Of the nine species with both a readable residue-96 window and a published
call, five respond to ingested dsRNA and four do not, and the sequon does not
separate them: C. briggsae, C. remanei, C. brenneri and C. tropicalis carry the
intact N94-x-[ST] motif and are insensitive, while C. elegans, C. kamaaina,
C. portoensis and C. wallacei carry it and respond. C. afra, which has lost
Asn94 and is therefore a naturally occurring equivalent of the N94A construct,
responds. Residue 96 is conserved across the genus but does not predict
environmental RNAi competence, which agrees both with the N94A editing result
and with Nuez & Felix's own inference of at least four independent gains or
losses of environmental RNAi within Caenorhabditis.

ROW ORDER AND THE REFERENCE TOPOLOGY. Species in the alignment figure are
ordered on the Open Tree of Life induced subtree for these taxa (synthetic tree
opentree16.1, 2025-12-20, taxonomy version 3.7draft3), retrieved from the
tree_of_life/induced_subtree endpoint and deposited as Newick. It is topology

SIGNIFICANCE FOR THE EDITING SERIES. Each construct was compared with the wild
type of its own background (JU1793[96T] and JU2466_A[96K]) by Fisher's exact
test on hatched and unhatched counts. The direction is reported alongside each p
value because the N94A construct in the JU1793 background hatches HIGHER than
wild type (99.3% against 94.8%, p = 0.004), which a p value alone would not
convey. Each genotype is a single plate, so each test compares one plate with
one plate.

The conclusion this supports is bounded: residue 96 is serine or threonine
across Caenorhabditis and lysine in none of the species whose window can be
read, so 96K is derived within C. elegans. It is not conservation across
nematodes -- one genus out, the protein is not alignable at all. No functional claim follows from the conservation, because the
glycosylation hypothesis for this sequon was tested with N94A and failed (see
the allele-swap section).

Re-running the search requires the 52 whole proteomes, roughly 290 MB, which are
public releases rather than data generated here and are not deposited.
scripts/make_sid2_ortholog_tables.py records the proteome accessions, the search
parameters and the resulting per-species best hits, and rebuilds the deposited
tables from sequences fetched by accession; it asserts the conservation counts
above so a change in the underlying sequences fails loudly.

Population missense variation in sid-2 was taken from the BCSQ-annotated CeNDR
release across the 540 isotypes: 17 missense variants in the 3.0 kb span, of
which only two fall at a position C. briggsae conserves, T96K being one. T96 is
the conserved state and 96K is the common derived variant at 46% allele
frequency. XZ1516 differs from N2 at seven of the eight curated sites (78, 96,
141, 144, 151, 153 and 209), so it carries a diverged sid-2 haplotype rather
than a single variant of interest.

INTERVAL CENSUSES. Two intervals were censused completely rather than searched:
the 37 kb the NIL series resolves (chromosome III 13,657,700-13,695,000;
scripts/make_nil_interval_tables.R, drawn as SUPP_FIG_XX_nil_interval_genes)
and a 100 kb window on each independent HT115-vs-mig-6 cross QTL above LOD 500
(scripts/make_mig6_locus_tables.R, four loci). Gene models come from the
WormBase WS283 csq GFF3; parental genotypes and consequences from the
BCSQ-annotated CeNDR release (20231213). Every site at which the two parents of
the relevant cross differ is kept, not only the protein-altering ones, because
the denominator is what makes the count interpretable.

bcftools csq emits consequence terms rather than the HIGH/MODERATE/LOW/MODIFIER
impact ladder that snpEff and VEP report, so the mapping is stated rather than
assumed: HIGH is stop_gained, stop_lost, start_lost, frameshift,
splice_acceptor and splice_donor; MODERATE is missense and inframe indels; LOW
is synonymous, splice_region, start_retained and stop_retained; everything else
is MODIFIER. Where a site carries several comma-separated consequences the most
severe entry is taken and its own gene, transcript and residue fields are read
from that entry, so the reported consequence always belongs to the impact class
reported beside it.

Two things bound what a census can conclude, and both are reported per interval.
Per-parent no-call counts, because a site uncalled in either parent cannot be
compared: 1 of 959 sites in the NIL interval, but 985 of 4,464 in N2 at the
chromosome I mig-6 locus against 133 in XZ1516, so the same claim is far
stronger at some loci than others. And overlap with the CeNDR per-strain
divergent regions, which mark where a wild isolate's reads stop aligning well
enough to call; none of the five windows overlaps one for the relevant parents,
so the divergent-region track does not explain the missingness above. What
these call sets cannot see at all is structural variation, since they are
short-read SNVs and small indels.

In the NIL interval this gives 27 parental differences across twelve genes, of
which two alter a protein and both are missense in sid-2 (V5L and T96K), with no
HIGH-impact difference anywhere. Across the four mig-6 windows -- 86
protein-coding genes in 400 kb -- there is likewise no HIGH-impact difference,
and 7 to 21 protein-altering differences per window spread across many genes,
which is what an unfine-mapped peak looks like beside a fine-mapped one.

PROTEIN TOPOLOGY AND STRUCTURE PREDICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SID-2 topology was predicted with DeepTMHMM, which calls a signal peptide at
residues 1-20, an extracellular region at 21-193, a single transmembrane helix
at 194-211 and a cytoplasmic region at 212-311. The UniProt annotation for
G5EEV9 gives slightly different boundaries (signal 1-20, extracellular 21-188,
transmembrane 189-209, cytoplasmic 210-311); figures state which boundary set
they use.

The SID-2 dimer was predicted with AlphaFold3 (five models). [TO FILL: whether
the AlphaFold Server or a local installation was used, and the date and model
version.] Model confidence limits what the prediction can be used for: ipTM is
0.45-0.46 and pTM 0.46-0.48 across all five models, with 43-46% of the model
called disordered, so the dimer interface is not credible, is not shown in any
figure and dimerisation is not claimed. Mean pLDDT by region for chain A is 56.4
(signal peptide), 72.3 (extracellular), 46.0 (transmembrane helix) and 40.0
(cytoplasmic); only the extracellular domain reaches the confident band, and it
alone is displayed. The variant neighbourhood is locally confident: N94 70.5,
C95 78.0, T96 79.3.

A separate AlphaFold3 run of the trimmed ectodomain with double-stranded RNA
gave better protein-protein confidence (ipTM 0.56-0.58, 7-15% disordered) but
protein-RNA chain-pair ipTM of only 0.22-0.48 across the five models, so the RNA
placement is not reproducible; it was run for the 96K allele only and is not used
in any figure.

Molecular docking of double-stranded RNA was attempted with HADDOCK and is not
reported. Those runs returned positive HADDOCK scores (+98 to +200, where
favourable is negative), interaction energies of order 1e4, restraint-violation
energies of 480-1380 and 4-13 structures per cluster, and the two alleles were
run against different 91-residue ambiguous-restraint sets, so they are not
comparable even in principle.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECONDARY STRUCTURE, ORIENTATION AND DISTANCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Secondary structure was assigned from the model backbone using the Kabsch and
Sander (1983) electrostatic hydrogen-bond criterion, implemented directly
because no DSSP binary was available (scripts/sid2_ribbon_render.py). The amide
hydrogen was placed 1 A from N along the C(i-1)->O(i-1) direction, as DSSP does;
the hydrogen-bond energy was taken as

  E = 332 x 0.42 x 0.20 x (1/r_ON + 1/r_CH - 1/r_OH - 1/r_CN)   kcal/mol

with a bond called at E < -0.5. Alpha helices were assigned from two consecutive
4-turns and strands from residues participating in a parallel or antiparallel
bridge; 3-10 and pi helices were folded into helix, and isolated single-residue
assignments were discarded. The assignment is independently corroborated: it
calls residues 194-211 100% helix, exactly the span DeepTMHMM identifies as the
transmembrane helix, and 60% helix across the signal peptide. The extracellular
domain is beta-rich (46% strand, 3% helix) and T96 lies within a strand, with
N94 in the preceding loop.

For display, the model was used in the membrane frame produced by earlier
orientation work, in which z = 0 is the bilayer centre and +z is the intestinal
lumen (tilt 32.5 degrees to the membrane normal, hydrophobic thickness 32.4 A,
transmembrane helix spanning z = -13.2 to +13.2 A). Residue 96 sits at z = +62 A,
on the lumenal face.

Two classes of annotated residue were measured against residue 96, and they are
kept separate because they are different kinds of evidence.

McEwan, Weisman & Hunter (2012) identify SID-2's three extracellular histidines
as H32, H168 and H175 and test exactly those, with His->Ala and His->Glu each
reducing dsRNA transport. The modelled ectodomain (residues 21-188) contains
exactly three histidines, at 32, 168 and 175, which confirms the numbering
independently of any sequence database. D34 is a separate line of evidence: it
is the qt13 loss-of-function allele, not one of the histidines, and residue 34
is an aspartate. An earlier version of this section grouped the two together as
"residues with a published effect on dsRNA uptake" and, being drawn from a
UniProt-derived list, also carried a residue 199 that lies in the transmembrane
helix and does not belong to the ectodomain at all.

C-alpha distances from residue 96: H32 16.4 A, H168 19.2 A, H175 37.8 A, and
D34 13.5 A, against a median C-alpha distance from residue 96 of 22.3 A across
the other 167 residues of the modelled ectodomain (21-188). Nearest heavy-atom distances are shorter (D34 9.9, H32 14.6,
H168 16.9 A).

NO PROXIMITY CLAIM IS MADE, and the corrected residue set is why. 41.9% of the
ectodomain (70 of the other 167 residues) lies within 20 A of residue 96, so two
of the three histidines falling inside that radius carries a binomial
probability of 0.38 -- weaker than the 0.19 that the earlier four-residue set
gave, and that earlier figure was computed over a set that should not have
included D34. All of these are recomputed by
scripts/sid2_local_charge_table.py, which prints them, rather than being
carried as prose. The co-localisation is
reported as spatial context only. The argument that Figure 4C does make is about
charge, below, which does not depend on proximity to the annotated residues.

[Note: D34 is 13.5 A from residue 96 in the membrane-oriented ectodomain file
and 13.8 A in the full-length AlphaFold model, because the two files are
different exports of the same prediction; figures state which file they use.]

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ECTODOMAIN CHARGE AS A FUNCTION OF pH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Net charge of the extracellular domain (residues 21-193: 11 Asp, 7 Glu, 3 His,
4 Cys, 5 Tyr, 9 Lys, 1 Arg) was computed by Henderson-Hasselbalch over the side
chains with pKa values Asp 3.90, Glu 4.07, His 6.04, Cys 8.18, Tyr 10.46, Lys
10.54 and Arg 12.48. Termini were omitted, because the ectodomain is not a free
polypeptide -- it continues into the transmembrane helix -- so a free
alpha-carboxylate would be an artefact of where the domain was cut.

The domain carries -8.4 e at pH 7.4 but only -0.2 e at pH 4.4, and its
isoelectric point, 4.38, is close to the pH of the intestinal lumen. Adding the
+1 e of T96K takes the surface from -0.2 e to +0.8 e at pH 4.4 and moves the
isoelectric point to 4.50. This is presented as a hypothesis-framing
calculation, not as an estimate of binding: no affinity, free energy or
occupancy is computed anywhere in this work.

LOCAL NET CHARGE, AND WHERE T96 SITS IN IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The whole-domain figure above averages over a surface that is not uniform, so
the same Henderson-Hasselbalch calculation was applied locally. For each residue
of the modelled ectodomain (21-188), the side-chain charges of every ectodomain
residue with a C-alpha within 12 A were summed, giving a per-residue local net
charge. pKa values are the set used above. Computed by
scripts/sid2_local_charge_table.py, which writes the per-residue table to
supplemental_data/structure/ and prints every number quoted here.

At pH 4.4 the domain is net +0.47 e across 21-188 (against -8.34 e at pH 7.4;
the -0.2 e above is for 21-193, so the two differ by the five residues the
membrane-oriented file does not carry -- quote the 21-193 figure in the text and
this one only for the local analysis).

T96's own neighbourhood is +1.24 e, the 82nd percentile of the 168 ectodomain
residues, whose median is 0.00. Substituting lysine adds +1 e to give +2.24 e,
the 98th percentile. The pocket is made by two lysines, K93 with its C-alpha at
6.6 A and K132 at 6.8 A (4.5 and 4.4 A nearest heavy atom). T96 is
solvent-exposed at the domain median, SASA 62.4 A^2, the 48th percentile, so the
side chain is available to solvent rather than buried.

The three uptake histidines sit at -0.15 (H32), +0.85 (H168) and +1.46 e (H175).
H175, which is 37.8 A from T96, is in the most positive local environment of the
three.

TWO BOUNDS ON THE INTERPRETATION. 81 of the 168 residues have positive local
charge at this pH, so a positive pocket by itself is unremarkable; the claim is
the percentile, not the sign. And the domain is net acidic at neutral pH, so
this is a basic pocket in an otherwise acidic domain, not a polybasic surface.

WHY CHARGE IS THE RELEVANT AXIS AT ALL. McEwan, Weisman & Hunter (2012) found
that individual His->Arg substitutions retained more function than His->Ala but
did not reach wild type, while the TRIPLE His->Arg mutant internalised more
dsRNA than wild type. Replacing pH-dependent positive charge with permanent
positive charge increased uptake. T96K adds permanent positive charge to the
same lumenal domain and increases RNAi sensitivity in all three backgrounds
tested here, so the published experiment and ours point the same way.

This remains a hypothesis with a direction, not a demonstration. Charge counting
is formal and ignores pKa shifts, local dielectric and glycan shielding (three
of the nine N-glycosylation sequons lie in the ectodomain); whether SID-2
contacts dsRNA at all is not established by anything here; and the directional
agreement is a correlation across three backgrounds rather than evidence that
charge is the mechanism. The discriminating experiment is charge-matched: T96R
should behave like T96K if charge is the mechanism, and T96Q, isosteric and
neutral, like the wild type.

STATISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Binomial proportions are reported with Wilson score 95% intervals, which behave
correctly near 0 and 1 where the Wald interval does not. Differences of two
proportions are reported with Newcombe's hybrid-score interval, which is built
from the two Wilson intervals and inherits their behaviour at the boundaries.
Counts were compared with Fisher's exact test. Rank correlations are Spearman's rho; the plate
phenotype score is a six-level ordinal scale, so rank correlation is used for
every comparison involving it. Two-group comparisons of continuous phenotypes
use the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with Welch's t test reported alongside where the
two disagree, and both are reported rather than the more favourable one.
Permutation tests are stated with their number of draws. No correction for
multiple comparisons was applied within the small hatching experiments; the
genome-wide thresholds are given above.

EFFECT SIZES ACROSS EXPERIMENTS. The NIL series and the allele swaps do not
share a scale: JU2466 hatches 0.357 under pos-1 in the first and 0.045 in the
second, the same strain at the same nominal dose, a 7.9-fold difference, while
JU1793 gives 0.994 against 0.948. Raw percentage-point effects are therefore not
comparable between the two experiments, and every cross-experiment effect is
reported both raw and normalised to the JU1793-JU2466 span measured within its
own experiment, on which 0 is JU2466 and 1 is JU1793 and all contrasts are
signed as the cost of carrying the JU2466 allele
(scripts/effect_size_ladder.R). Additivity of two introgressed segments is
tested by comparing the doubly-introgressed line against the hatching predicted
from each segment's own single-segment effect.

The N2 allele-swap experiment is reported raw only, with no normalised value.
It contrasts one background's two alleles at residue 96 -- N2 carrying 96T
against two independently edited 96K lines, pooled -- across 0, 25, 50, 75 and
100% pos-1 food, and it has no second parental genotype, so there is no span of
the kind used above and filling that column would put a different quantity
beside the ones already in it. At 25% food the effect is +0.282 [0.221, 0.348]
in hatched fraction -- 32.3% hatching for unedited N2 against 4.0% for the two
96K lines pooled -- which falls between the two JU backgrounds' raw effects of
+0.139 and +0.417. The effect exists only at that dose: it is -0.007 at 0%,
+0.283 at 25%, +0.026 at 50%, +0.007 at 75% and -0.007 at 100%, because N2
carrying 96T is already down to 0.060 hatching by 50% food and there is no room
left above 25% for the allele to matter. The 0% row is the negative control.
That dose difference also means the N2 effect is not strictly comparable even
raw with the JU experiments, which were run at a nominal 50%.

EXPRESSION AND eQTL AT THE CENSUSED LOCI. The coding-variant censuses were
complemented by asking, for every protein-coding gene in the five windows,
whether the two parents of the relevant cross differ in its expression and
whether it carries a mapped eQTL in the 207-isolate expression study
(scripts/candidate_eqtl_expression.R). All four parents are among the 207, so
the comparison is direct. Of 75 coding genes, 9 carry a mapped eQTL and 5 of
those are local. Because the study provides one value per strain, a parental
difference has no within-strain replication and no p-value; it is reported as a
z-score against the 207-strain distribution, describing how unusual the gap is
rather than whether it is significant. Two further limits are stated with the
result: the expression data are whole-animal at one stage under one condition,
so a difference confined to the relevant tissue or to RNAi food would appear
null; and an eQTL in that panel describes wild variation in general, not these
two strains, so a gene may differ between the parents without a mappable eQTL
and vice versa. sid-2 serves as the positive control and behaves as it should --
z = 0.36 with no eQTL, i.e. no parental expression difference, consistent with
its effect being the T96K coding change confirmed by allele swap.

Allele-frequency shifts from the bulk-segregant crosses are NOT converted onto
the hatching scale. A shift in a selected pool is a selection response, and
converting it requires the selection intensity and the number of generations,
which are not recorded; cross QTL are therefore compared with the hatching
experiments on direction and rank only.
