STRAINS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Wild isolates were obtained from the C. elegans Natural Diversity Resource (CeNDR); genotypes throughout use the CeNDR 20210121 release. [TO FILL: husbandry -- growth medium, temperature, bleaching/synchronisation, food source for maintenance.]

Strains, alleles and oligonucleotides constructed for this work are listed in full in supplemental_data/Table_S_strains.csv and Table_S_oligos.csv. Strain designations are QX2545-QX2578, introgressions qqIR59-qqIR80, and sid-2 alleles qq212-qq223.

NIL construction. Near-isogenic lines carrying chromosome III right-arm introgressions between JU1793 and JU2466 were built in three stages. JU1793 and JU2466 were crossed, F2 progeny were singled into 96-well plates, and recombinants were identified by PCR across the interval with the length- polymorphic marker pairs oZ318/oZ319 (III:11.83 Mb), oZ326/oZ327 (12.18 Mb), oZ330/oZ331 (12.83 Mb) and oZ334/oZ335 (13.28 Mb). Recovered F3 progeny were singled and re-genotyped to identify homozygous recombinants, which were backcrossed to the recurrent parent six times and homozygosed. This produced the first-round lines, including QX2545 (wSZ153, qqIR59). QX2545 was crossed again to JU1793 and screened the same way to give the second round, including QX2552 (wSZ167, qqIR66).

Once the introgressed segment was too small for further recombination to be recovered by crossing, it was reduced by Cas9-induced recombination: a double- strand break was placed inside the segment and repaired from the homologue. Guide gSZ169 (III:13,657,684) applied to QX2552 gave QX2557 (wSZ176, qqIR71). Guides gSZ177 (III:13,695,000) and gSZ179 (III:13,736,877) applied to QX2557 gave the final series, including QX2561 (wSZ191, qqIR75) and QX2566 (wSZ196, qqIR80). Products of this step were genotyped with the RFLP marker pairs oZ440/oZ441 (DraI, III:13,686,395) and oZ434/oZ435 (HpyAV, III:13,773,217) together with oZ370/oZ371 (13.70 Mb). Introgression boundaries were confirmed by whole-genome sequencing for six lines and are in data/nil_ranges.bed; the remainder are bounded by the flanking markers only, and Table_S_strains.csv records which is which in its breakpoint_source column.

Genome editing at sid-2. All point edits used guide gSZ182, which cuts near III:13,680,248, the site distinguishing JU1793 (C, 96T) from JU2466 (A, 96K). Four 169 bp single-stranded repair templates were used, sharing one backbone:

  oZ446   installs 96K on a 96T background       (JU1793 and N2 edits)
  oZ456   installs 96T on a 96K background       (JU2466 edit)
  oZ490   N94A in JU1793, leaving 96T            (A-x-T sequon)
  oZ491   N94A in JU2466, leaving 96K            (A-x-K sequon)

oZ446 and oZ456 differ from the backbone at the residue-96 codon only. oZ490 and oZ491 change the N94 codon (AAC to GCC), which removes the N-x-S/T glycosylation sequon at N94-C95-T96, and additionally carry a synonymous substitution that blocks re-cutting; each leaves residue 96 as its own background carries it, so they test the sequon independently of the residue-96 identity. [TO FILL: injection method (ribonucleoprotein or plasmid), co-conversion marker, concentrations, and how many independent lines were recovered and outcrossed.]

Injected animals were singled 16 h after injection. Roller F1 progeny were singled and allowed to lay for 24 h, at which point the F1 parent was lysed for DNA. The edit site was amplified with oZ461/oZ468 and screened by HpyCH4IV digestion, and candidates were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Edited strains carried forward are QX2570 (wSZ200) and QX2571 (wSZ201), sid-2(qq215[T96K]) and sid-2(qq216[T96K]) in JU1793; QX2573 and QX2574 (wSZ203, wSZ204), two independent N2 lines carrying sid-2(qq218[T96K]) and sid-2(qq223[T96K]); QX2576 (wSZ206), sid-2(qq220[K96T]) in JU2466; and QX2577 and QX2578 (wSZ208, wSZ209), sid-2(qq221[N94A]) and sid-2(qq222[N94A]) in JU2466 and JU1793. Lines recovered carrying frameshifts or deletions rather than the intended substitution are listed in the strain table and were not used.

POOLED RNAi COMPETITION EXPERIMENT AND PHENOTYPES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A pool of wild isolates was grown on bacteria expressing double-stranded RNA against each of ten target genes (fog-2, mig-6, pos-1, rde-3, ric-3, rpn-12, spe-19, spe-43, unc-39, vha-5) and on the HT115 empty-vector control, and sequenced at two timepoints. [TO FILL: pool composition and how it was assembled, generations of competition, timepoint definitions, replicate structure, DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Per-strain phenotypes were derived from timepoint-2 frequencies and are distributed in three parameterisations per target: delta_ctrl_<gene>_T2, the RNAi-minus-control change in pool frequency; log2fc_<gene>_T2, its log2 ratio; and vst_<gene>_T2, a variance-stabilised transform. All association mapping and all phenotype figures use the vst columns. Nine of the 93 strains have no vst value and were dropped, leaving n = 84 for association mapping. [TO FILL: the definition of the variance-stabilising transform. It is not reproducible from this repository -- only its output is distributed -- and the transform matters for the interpretation of effect sizes.]

The 2023 pos-1 experiment was analysed separately and only its exported end products are used here. Sample-level frequencies are distributed at three read-depth cutoffs; cutoff 5 was used throughout, as the cutoff that reproduces the shipped delta_ctrl_pos-1_T2. One strain, JU1793, appears twice per sample in that dataset -- non-negative least squares splitting one strain's abundance across two identical entries in the genotype reference -- so rows sharing a (strain, sample) key were summed before deltas were taken. The shipped vst values and the association scan were used as distributed and still carry the averaged JU1793 value.

Responsiveness of individual isolates was assessed two ways over the 231 isolates carrying a vst value (scripts/DIAG_pos1_responsiveness_test.R). At the panel level, the number declining in frequency under pos-1 relative to control was tested against an even split by binomial sign test. At the isolate level, strains absent from all four pos-1 pools while present in control were counted directly, since four identical values have no variance and are invisible to any t test; the remaining isolates were tested by a one-sample t test on the four replicate deltas against zero, with Benjamini-Hochberg control across the 231 tests at q < 0.05. The two together are reported as a floor rather than an estimate. Two alternative constructions -- a Welch test of four pos-1 pools against two control pools, and a test pooling the three read-depth cutoffs -- are tabulated alongside in plots/diagnostics/pos1_responsiveness_variants.tsv with a column recording whether their observations are independent. Pool frequencies are compositional, so any such proportion measures decline relative to the pool rather than an absolute per-strain response.

MANUAL PLATE PHENOTYPING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
pos-1 RNAi responses were scored by eye on agar plates for 191 wild isolates on a six-level ordinal scale, 0 = complete response (sensitive) through 5 = no response (resistant), deposited at supplemental_data/phenotypes/plate_scores_pos1.tsv. [TO FILL: plate preparation, bacterial dose, developmental stage at scoring, number of animals or plates per strain, and whether scoring was blinded.] The 93 strains carried into the pooled panel were chosen from these scores.

The plate score was compared against two independent measurements (scripts/SUPP_FIG_plate_vs_paaby_vs_pos1original.R), in both cases by Spearman correlation, the plate score being ordinal: against the pooled 2023 pos-1 response on the variance-stabilised scale over the strains the two share, and against Paaby et al. 2015 over the 19 strains shared with that study. For the latter, wells were restricted to the pos-1 clone and to those with at least one egg or larva, image-derived counts were used rather than manual ones, and embryonic hatching was taken per well as larvae over eggs plus larvae and averaged over the wells each strain contributes. Hatching rather than lethality is used so that both measures increase with resistance; it is the exact per-well complement, so this changes the sign of the rank correlation and nothing else.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STRAIN-FREQUENCY DECONVOLUTION FROM POOLED SEQUENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Strain frequencies were recovered from pooled sequence by non-negative least squares against a genotype reference matrix. Markers with any missing genotype call were dropped rather than zero-filled, because a zero is a real homozygous-reference call in this encoding. Writing G for the genotype matrix and a for the per-sample allele counts, the normal-equation inputs G'a and G'G were formed and solved per sample with RcppML::nnls, and each sample's solution was normalised to sum to one (scripts/Figure1_common.R, baugh_frequencies()).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VALIDATION OF THE DECONVOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The deconvolution was validated three ways: in simulation, where input frequencies and counts are both synthetic; on a designed DNA mixture, where the input is known and the counts are real; and against published independent measurements of the same samples.

Simulation. Each wild isolate was assigned a fitness from one of seven published C. elegans traits with validated QTL (Albendazole_q75.TOF, PC1, assay_norm, mtDNA_ratio, value, amsacrine_f.L1, etoposide_median.TOF), giving seven simulated populations of 327 strains. The trait value was the fitness, shifted by the trait's own minimum so fitness is non-negative; a strain with no value for a trait was assigned fitness zero and is absent from that trait's pool. Expected pooled allele frequencies were computed from the CeNDR genotype matrix, observed alt-allele counts were simulated by binomial sampling at 1, 3, 5, 10, 30, 50, 100 and 500x, and those counts were deconvolved and compared with the known input. Recovery is the r-squared of estimated against known frequency at each depth. It is reported over the strains PRESENT in each trait's pool: NNLS returns exactly zero for an absent strain because zero is a boundary of the feasible set, so including absent strains scores whether the method noticed a strain was missing rather than how precisely it measured the rest. The all-strains series is still computed, because the 2021 record is on that basis and reproducing it is the regression check on the figure.

The simulation is run from source under a seed in ten independent replicates (scripts/make_simulation_seeded.R, seed 20260909), and the figure reports the mean over replicates with a band spanning them. The design matrix passed to NNLS was G stacked on its own complement, rbind(G, 1 - G), so reference-allele counts enter as their own equations. The seven trait vectors are deposited at supplemental_data/deconvolution/simulation_fitness_traits.tsv.

The 2021 run is retained. Its estimates remain deposited and all of its r-squared are recomputable from them (scripts/simulation_recompute_r2.R, checked on every push). Its binomial draw was never seeded, so it can be recomputed against but not reproduced; the seeded replicated run was generated for that reason. To separate redraw noise from estimator choice, the seven-trait simulation was re-run from the CeNDR 20210121 panel (2,917,997 markers x 327 strains) in three replicates at all eight depths with three estimators applied to identical counts: mcrals.fcnnls on the raw design, mcrals.fcnnls on the normal equations, and RcppML::nnls on the normal equations. [TO FILL: whether the redraw belongs in Methods at all, or in a supplement.]

Designed DNA mixture. 174 wild isolates were divided into four sets (A-D). Genomic DNA from each set was pooled and sequenced both pure, three libraries per set, and as a seven-step titration of set B against set C. The titration was a two-fold doubling series of set B against a fixed 1 uL of set C made up to 10 uL with water, giving designed B fractions of 0.091, 0.167, 0.286, 0.444, 0.616, 0.762 and 0.865; total input DNA was not held constant, so input mass and B fraction are confounded across the series, and each dilution was prepared once, so pipetting error is unreplicated. Alt-allele counts were taken with GATK ASEReadCounter, markers were restricted to mean depth above 5 and below 100, high-frequency variants were recoded so the minor allele is counted throughout, sites with any missing genotype were dropped, and counts were deconvolved by NNLS (scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_dilution_validation.R).

Per-strain resolution was related to genetic similarity. A strain absent from a pool must be assigned zero frequency in that pool's libraries whatever the DNA input, so for each strain the mean frequency assigned across the nine libraries of the three sets it does not belong to was taken as an assumption-free error measure ("leakage"). Genetic similarity was summarised as identity-by-state to the closest other strain in the reference, over 231,615 variable markers sampled under a fixed seed. One isotype is assigned to two sets: JU1580 and JU1793 share the isotype JU1793 and were placed in sets B and D. The deconvolution operates at isotype level and returns a single frequency for the pair; it is counted in set B throughout.

Comparison against MIP-seq. Accuracy was assessed against published molecular inversion probe frequencies for the Baugh L1 starvation time course (Webster et al. 2022, eLife 11:e80204), across the 23 samples in which both measurements exist. Three strain counts appear and are not interchangeable: the deposited reference carries 102 strains, MIP-seq frequencies were published for 100, and 99 are common. N2 was excluded from all comparisons -- as the reference strain its genotype shares sites with every other strain in the pool, so its frequency is not identified on the same footing -- leaving n = 98. PB306 (a previous name for isotype ECA259) is a pool member the deposited reference cannot estimate, and is restored by grafting its column from the 2022 genotype matrix in the source project; the grafted 103-strain reference is used for the traits and the untouched 102-strain fit is retained alongside it (scripts/make_baugh_deposited_fits.R). Whether the three strains with no MIP-seq measurement should be dropped was tested by refitting with the reference cut to the 100 measured strains, and against three synthetic near-twins matched on identity by state (scripts/DIAG_baugh_reference_ablation.R); they are retained.

For each strain and replicate arm the rate of change in pool frequency was taken as the slope of the regression of frequency change on day, with day 1 as baseline and day 17 excluded, computed in closed form and averaged over the five replicate arms. Both platforms were put through one identical transformation -- platform_slopes() in scripts/Figure1_common.R applies the same baseline, the same exclusion and the same slope function to both columns, the only asymmetry being which column is read. This slope is NOT on the scale of the published Slope trait, which is fitted to log2-transformed frequencies normalised to day 1 with the intercept forced through the origin; rank correlations are unaffected but the slope values should not be compared numerically against the published trait. Agreement was quantified by Spearman correlation across strains and, separately, per sample across strains.

Uncertainty on the slopes was obtained by repeating the entire slope calculation inside each of 100 bootstrap replicates of the deconvolution, rather than by propagating a stored standard error, and the reported interval is the percentile interval across replicates; each replicate supplies its own baseline, so the covariance between baseline and later timepoints is preserved. The bootstrap resampled MARKERS with replacement, 100 replicates at seed 100, refitting from scratch within each replicate. That the array resamples the plotted estimator was verified at four points (scripts/SUPP_FIG_XX_bootstrap_propagation_checks.R). [TO FILL: the recovered script fits its point estimate on the complement-stacked design rbind(G, 1 - G) but its bootstrap on the plain G, so as written the published intervals belong to a different estimator from the published point estimates. Determine which design the archived Baugh run used.]

Two trait definitions are in use and are not interchangeable. The published phenotypes are log ratios against the baseline sample: PC1 is the first component of prcomp with scale and centre applied to log2(f_day / f_baseline) over the twenty replicate-by-day columns (days 1, 9, 13 and 17 across five arms), and Slope is the regression of that ratio on day over days 1, 9 and 13 only, so day 17 is in PC1 and out of Slope. Both were identified by refitting on each day subset and scoring against the deposited values (scripts/make_baugh_published_recipe_traits.R). Applying the published recipe to deconvolved frequencies requires a rule for zeros, because NNLS returns exact zeros where read-count ratios do not; frequencies are floored at 1/(4n), a quarter of an equal share, which states the resolution the deconvolution is assumed to have rather than deriving one from the smallest observed value. Both constructions reference the baseline sample. The difference-based construction used for Figure 1 -- frequency minus its day-1 value, regressed on day -- takes no logarithm and needs no floor or tuning parameter, which is the reason to prefer it. Variance-stabilising transforms (asinh(f/c), square-root, arcsine-square-root) were tested and are not used. Mapping inputs are assembled by scripts/make_baugh_mapping_traits.R, which emits both constructions for both platforms.

Sequencing-depth requirements were assessed by subsampling reads to 0.25, 0.5, 1, 3, 5 and 10x, rerunning the deconvolution and recomputing the traits (scripts/baugh_L1_DownSample_Counts.R), scored both as agreement between downsampled and full-depth frequencies and as recovery of the published traits. The two do not saturate at the same depth, so a depth requirement is not quoted from frequency agreement alone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MULTIPLE-TESTING THRESHOLDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two thresholds are drawn on every association panel: Bonferroni over every tested marker, which treats markers as independent and is over-conservative in panels carrying long haplotype blocks; and alpha = 0.05 divided by the effective number of independent tests estimated from the eigenvalues of the marker correlation matrix by the method of Li and Ji (2005), in which each eigenvalue lambda contributes 1{lambda >= 1} + (lambda - floor(lambda)).

Computing the M x M marker correlation matrix directly is infeasible at these marker counts, so its non-zero eigenvalues were obtained from the n x n matrix A = Zc Zc' / (n - 1), where Zc is the column-standardised genotype matrix; the two share their non-zero spectrum. Missing calls were mean-imputed per marker rather than dropped, because dropping any marker with a missing call removed about 40% of markers and changed the denominator; Bonferroni is computed over the same marker set. trace(A) = M was asserted at run time as a correctness check (scripts/eigen_independent_tests.R; accessor in scripts/gwas_thresholds.R). The thresholds applied are:

  panel              markers    M_eff   Bonferroni   Li & Ji
  pooled_RNAi_expt   322,010      732      6.81       4.17
  pos1_2023          464,045    1,972      6.97       4.60
                                          (-log10 p)

ADVANCED-INTERCROSS BULK-SEGREGANT (xQTL) CROSSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two crosses were performed between strains at opposite extremes of the pooled assay: N2 x XZ1516 and JU1793 x JU2466. These are advanced intercross lines, not F2 pools. Each cross was intercrossed for ten generations, with every generation after the third seeded from 10,000 to 15,000 worms. Ten to fifteen thousand generation-11 worms were transferred onto the RNAi or empty-vector control bacteria, defining timepoint 1; 10,000 to 15,000 of their generation-12 progeny seeded timepoint 2; and approximately 30,000 generation-13 progeny were sequenced. [TO FILL: DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing platform.]

Timepoint 2 is descended from timepoint 1 rather than being a parallel pool, so the two timepoints are serial points on one trajectory and the experiment contains one pool per condition per cross; the only independent replication in the cross data is one cross against the other. Ten generations of intercrossing expand the genetic map about fivefold relative to an F2 (Darvasi and Soller 1995); the shipped map spans 1,584 cM and is rescaled by map.expansion.factor = 0.2 to the 317 cM per-meiosis map of C. elegans wherever a per-meiosis rate is required. File names in the distributed exports carry the string "F2-2", which is the pipeline's label for the second timepoint and not a statement that the population is an F2.

Allele counts at parental marker sites were obtained with GATK ASEReadCounter, summed across sequencing runs, and phased onto the two parental haplotypes at genetic-map markers. Per-sample allele-frequency deviations and their standard errors were computed on a 10 kb grid by a moving kernel approximately 100 kb wide, so no feature narrower than that kernel is resolvable. Each pair of conditions was compared as a contrast, yielding a per-marker effect (contrast.beta), z statistic and LOD. Deviations are phased to a single parent per cross (N2 in the N2 x XZ1516 cross), so the sign of contrast.beta says which parental allele rose. LOD was computed in log space: the p column distributed with these exports is formed on the linear scale and underflows to exactly zero above |z| ~ 38.5, so LOD or neglog10p rather than p was used for everything quantitative. The genome-wide threshold applied is LOD 3.57, from qchisq(2 x 0.05 / 2000, df = 1, lower.tail = FALSE) / (2 ln 10) -- alpha = 0.05 over 2,000 effective tests, in the LOD convention the export pipeline uses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QTL SUPPORT INTERVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
These are support regions, not confidence intervals, and are reported as such. They were defined as a fractional LOD drop from each chromosome's own peak marker rather than a fixed number of LOD units, because an absolute drop wide enough for a strong peak spans an entire chromosome on a weak one. The shipped interval tables use a 10% drop; the interval tracks in Figure 2 use a 5% drop and are restricted to QTL with a peak LOD above 100. Secondary peaks on the same chromosome were masked with a 1 Mb buffer either side of a called interval and were required to reach 30% of the chromosome maximum (scripts/Figure2.R). A complete peak table admitting every peak above the genome-wide threshold, with its interval, parental frequencies and rank on its chromosome, was built separately (scripts/cross_qtl_full_summary.R); secondary peaks are admitted as independent QTL only where the LOD trough between them and the taller peak on the same chromosome falls below the genome-wide threshold, and are otherwise reported but flagged as shoulders.

Neither drop rule can be calibrated against the data, because timepoint 2 descends from timepoint 1 and the design holds no parallel replicate and no clean null. A null was therefore simulated (scripts/DIAG_cross_null_simulation.R): two inbred founders, ten generations of random intercrossing at N = 12,500, the split into two arms at generation 11, two further generations per arm with the last expanded to 30,000, and sequencing at the observed depth, scored through the pipeline's own estimator on a 10 kb grid. 200 null replicates were run, and 200 more at each of two QTL positions on chromosome III -- the sid-2 position at 13.68 Mb, nine grid loci from the chromosome end, and a mid-arm position at 6.90 Mb -- across selection coefficients of 0.15, 0.30, 0.60 and 1.00. Both positions are scored because a terminal QTL cannot be overshot outward and so flatters every rule. The threshold and coverage this yields are reported with the results; the intervals are retained as drawn.

Where a QTL is called in both crosses, two criteria are reported rather than one: interval overlap, the stricter, and a positional criterion, that the other cross's scan clears the genome-wide threshold at this peak's own position.

General against target-specific response loci. N2 x XZ1516 ran five knockdown pools against one shared HT115 control, so a locus can be classified by how many targets respond at it. Classification is on effect size with significance necessary only: a target counts as responding at a locus when its pool differs from HT115 by |dfreq| >= 0.10 with LOD above the genome-wide threshold, and a locus is general when at least three targets respond in the same direction (scripts/cross_qtl_condition_sharing.R). The cut is swept over |dfreq| >= 0.05, 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20 and both sweeps are reported. Two limits are stated with the counts: every contrast within a cross shares one HT115 pool, so drift in that pool imitates a general locus; and the two timepoints are serial points in one lineage rather than replicate pools.

NEAR-ISOGENIC LINES AND FINE-MAPPING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Introgression boundaries for the NIL series are in data/nil_ranges.bed. The interval resolved by the series is the region carried by QX2561 (wSZ191) and not by QX2566 (wSZ196). --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VARIANT ANNOTATION WITHIN QTL INTERVALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Variants differing between the parents of each cross were extracted within every support interval and annotated for consequence with bcftools csq (BCSQ) (scripts/pooled_cross_candidate_variation.R). Per-interval tables are distributed under plots/pooled_cross_intersection/. Consequences were graded into impact classes: HIGH is frameshift, stop gained or lost, start lost and splice acceptor/donor; MODERATE is missense, inframe indel and protein-altering; LOW is synonymous, splice_region, start_retained and stop_retained; everything else is MODIFIER. Where a site carries several comma-separated consequences the most severe entry is taken and its gene, transcript and residue fields are read from that entry, so the reported consequence always belongs to the impact class beside it.

Two limits are reported per interval: per-parent no-call counts, because a site uncalled in either parent cannot be compared; and overlap with the CeNDR per-strain divergent regions, which mark where a wild isolate's reads stop aligning well enough to call. These call sets are short-read SNVs and small indels and cannot see structural variation.

Allele assignment at the T96K site (chromosome III 13,680,248 C>A, REF C encoding 96T and ALT A encoding 96K) was verified against four strains whose allele is known independently: JU1793 and N2 carry 96T, JU2466 and XZ1516 carry 96K.

A third, incomplete cross, JU2466 x XZ1516, is reported as a diagnostic rather than a result: both parents carry 96K so T96K cannot segregate between them, but there is no HT115 control pool, every contrast is one RNAi condition against another, and only three timepoint-2 pools exist. Data are staged by scripts/make_jx_cross_chr3_tables.R and analysed by scripts/diagnostic_xz1516_sid2.R.

Expression and eQTL at the censused loci. For every protein-coding gene in the five windows, the two parents of the relevant cross were asked whether they differ in its expression and whether the gene carries a mapped eQTL in the 207-isolate expression study (scripts/candidate_eqtl_expression.R); all four parents are among the 207. The study provides one value per strain, so a parental difference has no within-strain replication and no p-value, and is reported as a z-score against the 207-strain distribution. Two limits are stated with the result: the expression data are whole-animal at one stage under one condition, so a difference confined to the relevant tissue or to RNAi food would appear null; and an eQTL in that panel describes wild variation in general rather than these two strains.

EMBRYO HATCHING ASSAYS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Strains were synchronised by hypochlorite treatment and grown until L4. Three days before plating onto RNAi bacteria, populations were amplified by transferring five L4s to each of three plates. After approximately 72 h, 10-15 L4s were plated onto each RNAi condition. The following day, 150 to 200 embryos were transferred to an unseeded NGMA plate and counted to determine the exact starting number; after 24 h the number of unhatched embryos was counted. Hatching was scored as the number of plated embryos that failed to hatch on a lawn of pos-1 RNAi bacteria diluted with HT115. [TO FILL: bacterial strain and induction, plate preparation, and who scored blind to genotype.]

The dilution differs between experiments and the numbers are not comparable across them:

  NIL series (Figure 3C and its supplement)      50% pos-1 RNAi
  JU1793 / JU2466 edits (Figure 4A, supplement)  50% pos-1 RNAi
  N2 edits (Figure 4B)                           25% pos-1 RNAi

The N2 edits were assayed across a 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% series, and 25% is reported because it is the only dose with dynamic range in that background (SUPP_FIG_XX_n2_swap_dose); the 0% row is the negative control. The dilution is not recorded in the source data files for the NIL series or the JU1793/JU2466 edits -- their condition column carries only "pos"/"ht115" -- so those doses come from the lab record. [TO FILL: confirm both 50% figures, and consider adding a dose column when those tables are next exported.]

Each strain was assayed on one plate per condition, so reported intervals are Wilson 95% binomial intervals on that plate's embryo count and describe counting uncertainty, not between-plate variability. Genotypes were compared with Fisher's exact test on hatched/unhatched counts. Odds ratios are the conditional maximum-likelihood estimate from Fisher's exact test with its exact conditional interval (R fisher.test), not the sample odds ratio with a Woolf interval. Whether the residue matters more in one background than the other is tested by a logistic model of hatching on background, allele and their interaction, reported as a ratio of odds ratios with Wald and likelihood-ratio p values; comparing two separately estimated intervals for overlap is not that test.

The JU2466 parent appears in ju_allele_swaps_hatching.csv as two isolates of the same genotype, JU2466_A and JU2466_B. The reported parental rate is JU2466_A alone, because the 96T edit QX2576 (wSZ206) was made in the A background, so A is the isogenic comparator and pooling would import a between-isolate difference into a within-isolate contrast; the Fisher test reported for this comparison is JU2466_A against QX2576.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROTEIN TOPOLOGY AND STRUCTURE PREDICTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SID-2 topology was predicted with DeepTMHMM. The UniProt annotation for G5EEV9 gives slightly different boundaries; figures state which boundary set they use.

The SID-2 dimer was predicted with AlphaFold3 (five models). [TO FILL: whether the AlphaFold Server or a local installation was used, and the date and model version.] Model confidence limits what the prediction is used for: the dimer interface is not credible, is not shown in any figure, and dimerisation is not claimed; only the extracellular domain reaches the confident band and it alone is displayed. Confidence metrics per region are reported with the structure figures. A separate run of the trimmed ectodomain with double-stranded RNA was made for the 96K allele only; its protein-RNA confidence is too low for the RNA placement to be reproducible and it is not used in any figure. Molecular docking of double-stranded RNA was attempted with HADDOCK and is not reported: the runs returned unfavourable scores and the two alleles were run against different 91-residue ambiguous-restraint sets, so they are not comparable even in principle.

Secondary structure was assigned from the model backbone using the Kabsch and Sander (1983) electrostatic hydrogen-bond criterion, implemented directly because no DSSP binary was available (scripts/sid2_ribbon_render.py). The amide hydrogen was placed 1 A from N along the C(i-1)->O(i-1) direction, as DSSP does. Per- residue local net charge, orientation relative to the membrane normal and inter-residue distances were computed from the same model; figures state the model and chain used.

STATISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The plate phenotype score is a six-level ordinal scale, so rank correlation is used for every comparison involving it. Two-group comparisons of continuous phenotypes use the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, with Welch's t test reported alongside where the two disagree, and both are reported rather than the more favourable one. Permutation tests are stated with their number of draws. Binomial proportions carry Wilson 95% intervals. No correction for multiple comparisons was applied within the small hatching experiments; the genome-wide thresholds are given above.

Effect sizes across experiments. The NIL series and the allele swaps do not share a scale -- the same strain at the same nominal dose gives different hatching in the two -- so raw percentage-point effects are not comparable between them. Every cross-experiment effect is therefore reported both raw and normalised to the JU1793-JU2466 span measured within its own experiment, on which 0 is JU2466 and 1 is JU1793 and all contrasts are signed as the cost of carrying the JU2466 allele (scripts/effect_size_ladder.R). Additivity of two introgressed segments is tested by comparing the doubly-introgressed line against the hatching predicted from each segment's own single-segment effect. The N2 allele-swap experiment is reported raw only: it contrasts one background's two alleles at residue 96 and has no second parental genotype, so there is no span of the kind used above, and it was run at a different dose from the JU experiments.

Allele-frequency shifts from the bulk-segregant crosses are not converted onto the hatching scale. A shift in a selected pool is a selection response, and converting it requires the selection intensity and the number of generations, which are not recorded; cross QTL are compared with the hatching experiments on direction and rank only.
